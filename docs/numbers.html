Chinese numbers, dates and time 数字 · 日期 · 时间

Everything you need to count, give a date, tell the time and pay in Chinese. Chinese numbers are very regular: once you know one to ten and four more characters, you can say any number.

二 èr or 两 liǎng?

Use 二 èr for counting, in phone numbers, in dates and inside bigger numbers (十二, 二十). Use 两 liǎng in front of a measure word: 两个人, 两本书, 两点 (two o'clock). Before 百 either is heard; before 千 and 万, 两 is more usual.

Telling the time

ChinesePinyinMeansNote
三点sān diǎn3:00点 = o'clock
两点liǎng diǎn2:00两, not 二
三点十分sān diǎn shí fēn3:10分 = minutes
三点零五分sān diǎn líng wǔ fēn3:05零 before single-digit minutes
三点半sān diǎn bàn3:30半 = half
三点一刻sān diǎn yí kè3:15一刻 = a quarter; 三刻 = 45 minutes
差五分四点chà wǔ fēn sì diǎn3:55差 = short of, five to four
早上七点zǎoshang qī diǎn7 in the morningThe part of the day comes first: 早上 上午 中午 下午 晚上
现在几点？xiànzài jǐ diǎn?What time is it?

Time words go before the verb, and the order is big to small: 我明天上午九点去学校。 (I go to school at nine tomorrow morning: literally I tomorrow morning nine o'clock go school.)
