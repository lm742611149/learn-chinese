A + 被 (+ B) + verb

The 被 passive puts the thing acted on first; the doer comes after 被 and can be vague, like 人 'someone'. For example, 书被人借走了 means 'the book was borrowed by someone'.

那本书被人借走了。 — That book has been borrowed by someone.
Seen in 借书
A 比 B + adjective (+ 多了)

比 compares two things; add 多了 for "much more": 地铁比公共汽车快多了。

今天比昨天冷多了。 — Today is much colder than yesterday.
Seen in 地铁真方便
A 比 B + adjective + 了很多 — much more than

比 compares two things. Putting 了很多 (or 多了) after the adjective shows the gap is big; never add 很 before the adjective in a 比 sentence.

家里用的水比以前多了很多。 — We were using a lot more water at home than before.
Seen in 节约用水
A 比 B + adjective + 多了

比 compares two things; adding 多了 after the adjective means 'much (more) ...', a big difference, e.g. 今天比昨天冷多了.

我觉得比昨天好多了。 — I feel much better than yesterday.
Seen in 发烧感冒
A 还是 B

还是 puts two choices in one question: 'A or B?'. It is used in questions, e.g. 你喝茶还是喝咖啡？

朋友问我南方好还是北方好。 — My friend asked me whether the south or the north is better.
Seen in 南方和北方
Adjective + 得 + description — so… that…

得 links an adjective to how strong it is: 高兴得像个孩子一样。

他累得不想说话。 — He was so tired he didn't want to talk.
Seen in 北京的四季
Adjective + 极了 — extremely…

极了 after an adjective makes it very strong: 漂亮极了。

今天热极了。 — It's extremely hot today.
Seen in 爬山
A没有用，B才有用 (only B is useful)

才 here means 'only then' — pairing 没有用 with 才有用 contrasts something useless with the one thing that actually helps.

担心没有用，努力才有用。— Worrying is useless, only hard work is.
Seen in 别担心
V + 了 + duration + 了 — has been doing for…

The second 了 shows the action is still true now: 中国人用筷子已经用了几千年了。

我学汉语学了两年了。 — I have been studying Chinese for two years (and still am).
Seen in 筷子的故事
V + 了 + 一遍 (to do something through once)

一遍 after a verb counts one complete pass through an action.

我又看了一遍，没有发现错误。
Seen in 电子邮件
V + 着 (an action continuing as a state)

着 after a verb shows the action's result continues, often describing manner.

同学们都带着小树，高高兴兴地去公园。
Seen in 环境和树
V1 + 着 + V2

着 after the first verb shows the manner or state in which the second action happens, e.g. 站着说话 'talk while standing'.

朋友笑着说：“太好了，你没迟到！” — My friend said with a smile: "Great, you're not late!"
Seen in 堵车
Verb + 得 + description — how well something is done

得 links a verb to a comment on how it is done; repeat the verb after the object: 你用筷子用得比我还好。

他汉语说得很好。 — He speaks Chinese very well.
Seen in 筷子的故事
Verb + 着 — a state that continues

着 after a verb shows a lasting state, not an action: 茶馆的门天天开着。

商店的灯还开着。 — The shop's lights are still on.
Seen in 茶文化
Verb + 着 — ongoing state or manner

着 shows a continuing state, often describing how another action is done: 妈妈笑着说……

他站着看书。 — He reads standing up.
Seen in 中秋节的月亮
adj + 地 + verb

地 turns an adjective into an adverb describing how an action is done.

他热情地说。
Seen in 迷路
adj + 多了

Placed after an adjective, 多了 means 'much more (adj)' — used for a big change compared with before.

现在觉得好多了。
Seen in 医院挂号
adj/verb + 得 + degree — describing the extent of a result

得 links an action or feeling to how extreme it was.

我高兴得差点儿哭出来。 — I was so happy I almost cried.
Seen in 手机丢了又找回
adjective + 地 + verb

地 turns an adjective into an adverb describing how an action happens, e.g. 慢慢地走 'walk slowly', 认真地学习 'study seriously'.

慢慢地，我越来越喜欢这里。 — Slowly, I came to like it here more and more.
Seen in 南方和北方
adjective + 极了 — extremely …

极了 comes after an adjective to mean 'extremely'. It replaces 很 or 非常, so never use them together.

房间里安静极了。 — The room was extremely quiet.
Seen in 难忘的生日
time word + 多少钱 (how much for...)

To ask the price for a period of time, put the time word right before 多少钱: 一个月多少钱？(How much per month?)

一个月多少钱？— How much is it per month?
Seen in 买手机卡
verb + 不到

不到 after a verb means the action cannot achieve its result: 找不到 'cannot find', 买不到 'cannot manage to buy'.

我找了很久，还是找不到。 — I searched for a long time and still couldn't find it.
Seen in 护照丢了
verb + 不懂

不懂 after 听 or 看 means you cannot understand what you hear or read: 听不懂 'can't understand (by listening)', 看不懂 'can't understand (by reading)'.

刚开始我几乎听不懂他们说的话。 — At first I could hardly understand what they were saying.
Seen in 南方和北方
verb + 了 + duration

To say how long an action lasted, put the length of time after the verb and 了, e.g. 我们等了半个小时 'we waited for half an hour'.

我们等了半个小时，车只走了一点儿。 — We waited half an hour and the car only moved a little.
Seen in 堵车
verb + 好 (resultative)

Adding 好 after a verb shows the action is finished successfully or brought to a good state, e.g. 饭做好了 'the food is ready'.

钱换好了，就等着去旅游了！ — The money is all exchanged; now I'm just waiting for my trip!
Seen in 换钱
verb + 得 + adverb/adjective — describing how an action is done

得 connects a verb to a phrase describing its manner or extent.

新郎和新娘穿得非常漂亮。 — The bride and groom were dressed beautifully.
Seen in 参加婚礼
verb + 着 — ongoing state or manner

着 after a verb shows a state that continues (放着 = is lying there) or how a second action is done (笑着说 = said with a smile).

桌子上放着他昨天买的药。 — The medicine he bought yesterday was lying on the table.
Seen in 照顾生病的朋友
verb + 过 — have ever done sth

过 after a verb marks past experience. In 我吃过的最好吃的蛋糕 the whole 过-phrase describes the cake: 'the best cake I have ever eaten'.

那是我吃过的最好吃的蛋糕。 — That was the best cake I have ever eaten.
Seen in 难忘的生日
verb/adjective + 得 + degree

得 after a verb or adjective introduces how far the action or state goes — its degree or result, e.g. 他跑得很快.

我的腿疼得不想走路。 — My legs ached so much that I didn't want to walk.
Seen in 锻炼身体
……以前，才…… (not until before X)

……以前 marks a point in time; 才 before the verb shows the action only happened at or right before that point, not earlier.

天黑以前，我们才回家。 — Before it got dark, we finally went home.
Seen in 动物园
……以后,就……

'After ..., (then) ...' — 以后 follows a time or action; 就 signals that the next thing happens right away.

十五分钟以后,我就拿到了新的银行卡。
Seen in 银行开户
……才 + verb

才 marks that something happens later than hoped or expected — 'not until'. It goes right before the verb, e.g. 他十点才起床.

那本书下星期一才会还回来。 — The book won't be returned until next Monday.
Seen in 借书换钱
……的时候

的时候 after a phrase means 'when/while ...', setting the time of the main event, e.g. 吃饭的时候别看手机.

我要去银行换钱的时候，突然发现护照丢了。 — Just when I was about to go to the bank to change money, I suddenly found my passport was lost.
Seen in 护照丢了
一 + measure word + 都/也 + 没/不 (not even one)

This pattern emphasizes 'not a single one': 一个人都没有 (not a single person), 一句话也不说 (not say a single word).

办公室里一个人都没有。— There wasn't a single person in the office.
Seen in 第一天上班
一 + verb1 + 就 + verb2

一…就… means 'as soon as A happens, B happens' — the second action follows the first immediately, e.g. 我一到家就给你打电话.

我一起床就觉得头疼。 — As soon as I got up, my head hurt.
Seen in 发烧感冒
一天吃三次 (take it three times a day)

To describe medicine dosage, say 一天 + 吃 + number + 次 for how often, then 一次 + amount for how much each time.

一天吃三次，一次两个。— Take it three times a day, two pills each time.
Seen in 在药店
一本一本地 (one by one) — reduplicated measure word + 地

Repeating a measure word before 地 shows an action done item by item, in order.

我一本一本地把书放回原来的位置。 — I put the books back one by one.
Seen in 第一次做志愿者
一点儿也不 + adjective — not … at all

一点儿也不 (or 一点儿都不) before an adjective or verb is a strong negative: 'not even a little'.

它一点儿也不害怕。 — It was not the least bit afraid.
Seen in 熊猫
一点儿都没 + verb (not even a little)

一点儿都 + 没 + verb emphasizes 'not even a little' — completely unchanged.

你一点儿都没变。— You haven't changed a bit.
Seen in 好久不见
一直 + verb

一直 means 'straight' with direction verbs, or 'continuously' with other verbs.

你一直向前走,到了红绿灯,再向右边走十分钟就到了。
Seen in 迷路
一边 A 一边 B — doing A and B at the same time

Two actions happening together: 我们一边吃月饼，一边看月亮。

他一边走路，一边听音乐。 — He listens to music while walking.
Seen in 中秋节的月亮
一边 A，一边 B — doing A and B at the same time

Two actions happening together: 我们一边吃饭，一边聊天。

他一边走路，一边听音乐。 — He listens to music while walking.
Seen in 学做中国菜
一边……一边……

一边 A 一边 B describes two actions happening at the same time, each 一边 followed by a verb, e.g. 他一边吃饭一边看电视.

我一边走一边想：钱换好了！ — As I walked, I thought: the money is all exchanged!
Seen in 换钱骑自行车请客吃饭熬夜的代价 +2
一边……一边…… (doing two things at once)

一边 + verb1，一边 + verb2 describes two actions happening at the same time.

小王才跑进来，一边说对不起，一边坐下。 — Xiao Wang finally ran in, saying sorry while sitting down.
Seen in 开会
一边……一边…… — doing two things at once

一边 A 一边 B says the two actions happen at the same time. Each slot takes a verb phrase, e.g. 一边走一边说.

我一边听音乐一边工作。 — I worked while listening to music.
Seen in 加班
一边……一边……(doing two things at the same time)

一边 before each of two verbs shows both actions happen together.

我一边写一边检查。
Seen in 电子邮件
一边…一边… (doing two things at once)

一边 + verb, 一边 + verb describes two actions happening at the same time: 一边刷牙，一边找衣服。

他一边吃饭，一边看手机。— He looks at his phone while eating.
Seen in 刷牙洗脸
一边…，一边…

Two 一边 clauses show two actions happening at the same time.

我一边走路，一边看手机。— I was walking while looking at my phone.
Seen in 小心
不但 A,还 B — not only… but also…

The second half must add MORE on top of the first: 不但会用筷子,还会做中国菜.

她不但会说汉语,还会写汉字。 — She can not only speak Chinese but also write characters.
Seen in 我的中国朋友
为了 + goal (in order to)

为了 introduces the purpose of the action that follows.

为了让环境变得更好，学校决定带大家去公园种树。
Seen in 环境和树
为了 + purpose — in order to

为了 introduces the goal of an action. It stands before the main clause, or right after 就是 to mean 'just in order to'.

很多人来中国，就是为了看熊猫。 — Many people come to China just in order to see pandas.
Seen in 熊猫
为什么不……呢? — why not…?

A friendly way to make a suggestion: 你为什么不坐地铁呢?

你为什么不早点儿睡呢？ — Why don't you go to bed a bit earlier?
Seen in 地铁真方便
了 marking a completed action

了 after a verb shows the action has happened or been completed.

我想了很多天，最后选择了离家近的工作。
Seen in 选择工作
什么时候……，什么时候……

Repeating a question word like 什么时候 turns it into 'whenever / whatever': the second clause happens at the same (unspecified) time as the first, e.g. 你什么时候来，我什么时候等你.

你什么时候有钱，什么时候还我。 — Pay me back whenever you have the money.
Seen in 还钱
你哪儿不舒服？(Where does it hurt?)

哪儿不舒服 literally asks 'where are you uncomfortable' — the standard way a doctor or pharmacist asks what's wrong.

你哪儿不舒服？— Where does it hurt?
Seen in 在药店
像……一样 (to be like...)

像 + noun + 一样 compares something to another thing, meaning 'just like...'.

老虎跑得很快，像风一样。 — The tiger ran very fast, like the wind.
Seen in 动物园
像…一样 (just like)

像 + noun + 一样 compares something to another: 像家人一样 (just like family), 像春天一样暖和 (as warm as spring).

我有一个像家人一样的邻居。— I have a neighbor who is just like family.
Seen in 邻居
先……再……

'First ..., then ...' — states the order of two planned actions.

我们先去宾馆,再去见客人。
Seen in 出差广告的秘密
先……，然后……

先…然后… links two actions in order: 'first ..., then ...'. Each part is a full verb phrase, e.g. 先吃饭，然后看书.

我先借了两本别的书，然后把名字写在借书卡上。 — I first borrowed two other books, then wrote my name on the library card.
Seen in 借书发烧感冒堵车
先…然后… (first…, then…)

先 + action 1, 然后 + action 2 puts actions in order: 先刷牙洗脸，然后吃早饭。

我们先做作业，然后看电视。— We do homework first, then watch TV.
Seen in 刷牙洗脸
先…，别… (first..., don't...)

先 puts one action before another, often paired with 别 or 不要 to say what not to do instead.

要先想办法，别一直着急。— Think of a solution first, don't just keep worrying.
Seen in 怎么办
再也不…了

再也不 + verb + 了 means 'never do something again' — a firm decision made after an experience.

我再也不看手机了。— I never look at my phone (while walking) again.
Seen in 小心
别 + verb (+ 了) — don't…

别 gives a friendly command not to do something: 别忘了放盐。

外面下雨了，别出去了。 — It's raining outside; don't go out.
Seen in 学做中国菜
别 + verb (don't...)

别 before a verb tells someone not to do something — 别担心 (don't worry) is one of the most common uses.

别担心，你已经很努力了。— Don't worry, you've already worked so hard.
Seen in 别担心
到现在还…… (even now still...)

到现在还 + verb shows that something continues to be true up to the present moment.

那场比赛真有意思，我到现在还记得。 — That match was really interesting — I still remember it to this day.
Seen in 有趣的比赛
又 A 又 B — both A and B

又…又… links two qualities or actions that are true at the same time. Both slots must be the same kind of word (two adjectives, or two verb phrases).

我又累又想家。 — I was both tired and homesick.
Seen in 难忘的生日搬家
又……又……

又 A 又 B links two qualities or results that are true at the same time: 'both A and B', e.g. 这个菜又便宜又好吃.

又锻炼了身体，又玩得很高兴。 — We both get exercise and have a great time.
Seen in 锻炼身体骑自行车请客吃饭外卖时代 +2
又……又……(both ... and ...)

又 before each of two adjectives or verbs shows both qualities or actions are true at once.

同事看到我又累又急。
Seen in 着急的一天
只好 (have no choice but to)

只好 shows the speaker is forced to take an action because there is no better option.

我只好打车去公司。
Seen in 着急的一天
因为 (because)

因为 introduces the reason for something; the clause it attaches to explains 'why'.

这次考试很重要，因为它能决定我的成绩。 — This exam is important, because it can decide my grade.
Seen in 担心考试
地 (adverbial marker)

Adjective + 地 + verb turns the adjective into an adverb describing how the action is done: 认真地回答 (answer seriously), 慢慢地走 (walk slowly).

我认真地回答了每个问题。— I answered every question carefully.
Seen in 面试
好久不见 (long time no see)

好久不见 is what Chinese speakers say when running into someone they haven't seen in a long time — 好久 means 'a long time' and 不见 means 'haven't seen each other'.

我们已经好久不见了。— We haven't seen each other in ages.
Seen in 好久不见
如果……，就……

如果 introduces a condition and 就 introduces the result: 'if ..., then ...'. For example, 如果明天下雨，我们就不去.

如果爬山那天下雨，我们就在城市里多玩一天。 — If it rains on the day of the climb, we'll spend one more day in the city.
Seen in 打算去旅行
如果……，就…… — if …, then …

如果 opens the condition. 就 belongs to the second clause and stands after its subject, right before the verb.

如果每个人都这样做，世界就会不一样。 — If everyone did this, the world would be different.
Seen in 节约用水
如果…就… — if…, then…

如果 introduces a condition, and 就 introduces the result that follows.

如果有朋友要来，就要提前告诉对方一下。 — If a friend wants to come over, let the other person know in advance.
Seen in 和室友相处
对……满意

'Satisfied with ...' — 对 introduces the target of the feeling.

客人对我们非常满意。
Seen in 出差
小心 (watch out / be careful)

小心 can stand alone as a warning — "Watch out!" — or be followed by a noun to mean 'watch out for ___': 小心车 (watch out for cars), 小心地滑 (careful, slippery floor).

走路的时候要小心车。— When walking, watch out for cars.
Seen in 小心
就在……的时候 (right when...)

就在 + clause + 的时候 marks the exact moment something happened, often right before another event.

就在比赛快结束的时候，一个队员突然踢进一个球。 — Right when the match was about to end, a player suddenly scored a goal.
Seen in 有趣的比赛
就要……了

就要…了 says something is about to happen soon, often with a time word before it, e.g. 火车就要开了 'the train is about to leave'.

还有两个星期我们就要去旅行了。 — In just two weeks we'll be going on our trip.
Seen in 打算去旅行
就要……了 (about to)

就要 + verb + 了 shows an action will happen very soon, often with a time word before it.

明天就要考试了，我希望没问题。 — Tomorrow I'm about to take the exam, and I hope there's no problem.
Seen in 担心考试
就要…了 (about to happen soon)

就要 + verb + 了 says something is going to happen very soon.

只有十分钟就要上课了。— Only ten minutes until class starts.
Seen in 怎么办
就要…了 (about to)

就要 + verb + 了 means something is going to happen soon, often with a time word: 下个月我就要上班了。

火车就要开了。— The train is about to leave.
Seen in 面试
希望 + clause (to hope that...)

希望 can be directly followed by a full clause describing what someone hopes will happen.

经理说，这个计划很重要，希望大家都努力做好。 — The manager said this plan was important, and hoped everyone would work hard to do it well.
Seen in 开会
并 + 不/没 — emphatic negation

并 strengthens a negative, meaning 'not at all' rather than a simple 'not'.

整个过程并不麻烦。 — The whole process wasn't troublesome at all.
Seen in 网购退货
快一点 / 快点 (hurry up!)

快 + 一点 (often shortened to 快点) is a common way to urge someone to move faster — usually said as a short, urgent command.

快一点，来不及了！— Hurry up, there's no time!
Seen in 快一点
快要……了 — about to; soon

Says something is going to happen very soon: 春节快要到了。

火车快要开了，我们快走吧！ — The train is about to leave; let's hurry!
Seen in 春节回家
怎么办？(what should I do?)

怎么办 asks what to do when you're stuck and don't know the solution — often said out loud when something goes wrong.

我的自行车坏了，怎么办？— My bike broke, what should I do?
Seen in 怎么办
手机卡 (SIM card)

手机卡 literally means 'phone card' — it's the everyday word for a SIM card. Buying one in China usually requires showing an ID or passport.

我需要买一张手机卡。— I need to buy a SIM card.
Seen in 买手机卡
才 — later or harder than expected

才 before a verb shows the result came late or took effort: 我看了三天，才买到一张票。

他晚上十二点才睡觉。 — He didn't go to bed until midnight.
Seen in 春节回家
打算 + verb (to plan to do something)

打算 introduces a plan or intention for the future.

他打算明年去中国旅游。
Seen in 网上聊天
把 (bǎ construction)

Subject + 把 + object + verb (+ other elements) moves the object before the verb to emphasize what happens to it.

今天早上，老师把这次考试的成绩发给了我们。 — This morning, the teacher gave us the results of this exam.
Seen in 成绩出来了
把 (bǎ) construction

Subject + 把 + object + verb + result moves the object before the verb to show what is done to it: 经理把我介绍给大家。

请把门开开。— Please open the door.
Seen in 第一天上班
把 (the 把 construction)

把 + object + verb moves the object in front of the verb to show what happens to it — common when handing something over: 把护照给他 (hand him the passport).

我把护照给他。— I gave him my passport.
Seen in 买手机卡
把 + object + verb + complement

The 把 sentence moves the object before the verb to show what you do to it; the verb needs a result or direction after it, e.g. 把门关好 'close the door properly'.

我把包里的东西都拿了出来。 — I took everything out of the bag.
Seen in 护照丢了
把 + object + verb + result

The 把 structure moves the object before the verb to show what is done to it, with a result after the verb.

我把衣服和电脑都放进了行李箱。
Seen in 出差共享单车
把 + object + verb + 上 — 把 with a result complement

把 puts the object before the verb when the action changes or disposes of it. Here 上 is a result complement meaning 'shut / closed'.

刷牙的时候也一定把水关上。 — When you brush your teeth, always turn the tap off.
Seen in 节约用水
把 + object + verb + 了一下 — the 把 construction

把 moves the object in front of the verb to show what you did to it. The verb cannot stand bare — it needs something extra such as 了一下, 完 or 好.

我先把房间打扫了一下。 — First I tidied the room up a bit.
Seen in 照顾生病的朋友
把 + object + verb + 出来 — expressing things out (result complement)

把 moves the object before the verb, and 出来 shows the result of the action bringing something out.

我们把各自的想法都说出来。 — We each spoke our own thoughts out loud.
Seen in 和室友相处
把 + object + verb + 给 + person

The 把 structure moves the object before the verb; 给 then introduces the person who receives the result.

我把计划发给了经理。
Seen in 电子邮件
把 + object + verb + 进/好

In a 把 sentence, a direction or result word like 进 or 好 follows the verb to show where the object ends up, e.g. 把书放进包里.

我把地图和照相机都放进了行李箱。 — I put the map and the camera into the suitcase.
Seen in 打算去旅行
把 + object + verb — disposal construction

把 moves the object before the verb to emphasize what happens to it.

我把包里的东西都找了一遍。 — I searched through everything in my bag.
Seen in 手机丢了又找回
把 + object + verb — the 把 sentence

把 moves the object before the verb to show what you do with it: 我把月饼送给老师。

请把门开开。 — Please open the door.
Seen in 送月饼
把 + object + 还给 + sb

In a 把-sentence the object moves before the verb to show what is being handled; 还给 'return to' then names the receiver, e.g. 请把书还给老师.

我把一千块钱还给了他。 — I gave the thousand yuan back to him.
Seen in 还钱
放心吧 (relax; rest assured)

放心吧 is a softer, more reassuring way to say 'don't worry' — often used to calm someone down.

放心吧，一定没问题。— Relax, it'll definitely be fine.
Seen in 别担心
数字 + 多 + 量词 (more than a number)

多 placed after a number and before the measure word shows the true amount is a bit more than that number.

我们聊了两个多小时。
Seen in 网上聊天
早点儿 + verb (do something sooner)

早点儿 before a verb suggests doing it a bit earlier, rather than waiting.

身体不舒服，要早点儿去药店买药。— When you're not feeling well, go to the pharmacy sooner rather than later.
Seen in 在药店
是……的 (emphasizing how/when/where)

是……的 is used for a past action to emphasize a specific detail, such as how, when, or where it happened.

你是怎么考得这么好的？ — How did you do so well on the exam?
Seen in 成绩出来了
更 (comparative: more; -er)

更 is placed before an adjective to compare two things without using 比.

妈妈觉得钱多的工作更好。
Seen in 选择工作
有的……有的…… — some… others…

Talks about different members of a group: 有的月饼是甜的，有的里面还有鸡蛋。

有的人喜欢喝茶，有的人喜欢喝咖啡。 — Some people like tea; others like coffee.
Seen in 送月饼
来不及 vs. 来得及

来不及 means there isn't enough time left to do something; 来得及 means the opposite — there's still time. Both are often used with 了 or 还.

别着急，我们还来得及。— Don't worry, we still have time.
Seen in 快一点
正在 + verb (in the middle of doing)

正在 before a verb shows an action is happening right now, ongoing at that moment.

熊猫正在吃竹子，很可爱。 — The panda was eating bamboo — very cute.
Seen in 动物园
比 + A + adj + 多了 (much more ... than A)

比 A + adjective + 多了 compares two things, saying one is much more so than the other.

点外卖比自己做饭快多了。— Ordering delivery is much faster than cooking myself.
Seen in 点外卖
比 … 多了 (much more … than)

A + 比 + B + adjective + 多了 makes a strong comparison: 我比以前年轻多了 (I look much younger than before).

今天比昨天热多了。— Today is much hotter than yesterday.
Seen in 减肥
比……还要…… (even more than)

比 compares two things; adding 还要 before the adjective strengthens the comparison to 'even more'.

我的成绩比我想的还要好。 — My grade was even better than I expected.
Seen in 成绩出来了
点外卖 (to order delivery)

点 means 'to order' and 外卖 means 'takeout/delivery food' — together, 点外卖 is the everyday phrase for ordering food to be delivered.

我用手机点了一个外卖。— I used my phone to order delivery.
Seen in 点外卖
疑问词 + 都/也 (question word for 'any/every')

A question word + 都/也 + (不/没) means 'everyone/anyone', 'everything/anything': 谁都不认识 (not know anyone), 什么都想吃 (want to eat everything).

那时候我谁都不认识。— At that time I didn't know anyone.
Seen in 邻居
着…就… (do one thing, then right away another)

着 marks an action still in progress; adding 就 + another verb shows what happens immediately after, often in a hurry.

我拿着包就往外面跑。— I grabbed my bag and ran straight outside.
Seen in 快一点
终于 + verb — finally

终于 goes before the verb and marks a result that came after a long wait or effort. Use 然后 or 后来, not 终于, when you are simply listing steps.

我们等了半个小时，终于看见了一只小熊猫。 — We waited half an hour and finally saw a little panda.
Seen in 熊猫
虽然 A，但是 B — although A, B

Both halves must appear in Chinese, unlike English where 'although' alone is enough.

虽然整理书架很累，但是我觉得很有意义。 — Although organizing shelves is tiring, I find it meaningful.
Seen in 第一次做志愿者爬山加班
虽然……但是…… (although...but...)

虽然 introduces a fact that seems to go against what follows; 但是 introduces the actual result, giving a contrast.

虽然我还是有点儿害怕，但是我决定好好休息，不再看书了。 — Although I'm still a little scared, I decided to rest well and stop studying.
Seen in 担心考试
虽然……但是……(although ... but ...)

Introduces a fact that contrasts with what follows, showing a concession.

虽然这一天很着急，但是最后很顺利。
Seen in 着急的一天选择工作
让 + person + verb — to have someone do something

让 introduces who is being asked or caused to do an action.

客服让我把大衣重新包好，寄回去。 — Customer service had me wrap the coat again and mail it back.
Seen in 网购退货
让 + sb + verb

让 means 'to have / ask someone to do something', introducing the person before the action.

她让我写名字,还让我想一个密码。
Seen in 银行开户
谁……都 + 不/没

A question word + 都 + negative means 'no one / nothing at all'.

谁的钱都不要!
Seen in 请客吃饭
越 A 越 B — the more A, the more B

Shows one thing growing along with another: 我越喝越觉得好喝。

雨越下越大。 — The rain is getting heavier and heavier.
Seen in 茶文化
越来越 (more and more)

越来越 + adjective describes something increasing over time: 越来越好 (better and better), 越来越冷 (colder and colder).

我的身体越来越好。— My health is getting better and better.
Seen in 减肥
越来越 + adjective

越来越 means 'more and more', describing a quality that keeps growing over time, e.g. 他汉语说得越来越好.

我的身体也越来越健康。 — I was getting healthier and healthier.
Seen in 锻炼身体还钱骑自行车迷人的丽江
越来越 + adjective (more and more)

越来越 shows a quality is continuing to increase over time.

公园会越来越漂亮。
Seen in 环境和树
越来越 + adjective — more and more…

越来越 shows a quality increasing over time.

气氛越来越热闹。 — The atmosphere got livelier and livelier.
Seen in 参加婚礼搬家加班
跟……一起 + verb — to do sth together with sb

跟 introduces the person you do something with, and 一起 sits right before the verb. 和 can replace 跟 with no change in meaning.

下午我跟他一起去医院检查。 — In the afternoon I went to the hospital with him for a check-up.
Seen in 照顾生病的朋友
还是 (still)

还是 before an adjective or verb shows that a situation hasn't changed — it's still the same as before.

到了最后五分钟，比分还是一样。 — In the last five minutes, the score was still tied.
Seen in 有趣的比赛
还是 (still; nevertheless)

还是 shows that despite something happening, a feeling or situation stays the same.

我们聊了两个多小时，还是觉得时间太短了。
Seen in 网上聊天
还是 + adj + 的 (still ...)

还是 before an adjective, with 的 at the end, says something remains in that state.

外卖还是热的。— The food was still hot.
Seen in 点外卖
还是那么 + adj (still just as...)

还是那么 says someone or something hasn't changed — still just as [adjective] as before.

你还是那么瘦。— You're still just as thin.
Seen in 好久不见
还没有 + verb (haven't yet)

还没有 (or 还没) before a verb shows that an action hasn't happened yet, even though it was expected.

会议开始的时候，小王还没有来。 — When the meeting started, Xiao Wang hadn't arrived yet.
Seen in 开会
