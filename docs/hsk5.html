What HSK 5 covers

HSK 5 works with a cumulative vocabulary of 2500 words — 1300 new ones on top of HSK 4, roughly CEFR C1. It covers society, economy, technology, education, tradition and modern change. The grammar that defines the level is written-register patterns, 之所以…是因为…, 与其…不如…, dense noun phrases, and idioms used precisely rather than decoratively.

The 51 readings on this page are written for exactly that boundary. Every one is original — no textbook passages — and stays inside the HSK 5 word list; anything above the level is listed in the vocabulary panel under the text. Each is 7-10 sentences in an essay register, built around one concrete example, with pinyin over every character, tap-to-translate, audio you can slow down, a three-question comprehension check and a short grammar note.

A routine that works: read once without looking anything up, then tap the words you missed, then listen and read along a second time. Five minutes a day beats an hour on Sunday.

HSK 5 questions

Is HSK 5 enough to study at a Chinese university?

It is the usual minimum for undergraduate admission, often alongside HSK 6 for competitive programmes. Reading speed matters more than the certificate: lectures assume you can process written Chinese without subvocalising.

Why does HSK 5 feel so much harder than HSK 4?

The vocabulary doubles again, and the register changes. HSK 5 texts are written Chinese, not spoken Chinese written down — shorter sentences carry more information and drop the connective padding.

How many new words should I add a day?

Fifteen to twenty is sustainable at this level, and only if you meet each one in context several times. Reading one text a day gives you that repetition without a separate flashcard session.
