HSK 6 · Fluent

怀旧为何流行

Huáijiù wèihé liúxíng
Why Nostalgia Is Popular · 194 words · ~3 min
近jìn几jǐ年nián，从cóng老lǎo歌gē翻红fānhóng到dào复古fùgǔ穿搭chuāndā盛行shèngxíng，怀旧huáijiù俨然yǎnrán成chéng了le一yì种zhǒng潮流cháoliú。
In recent years, from old songs becoming hits again to retro outfits sweeping the streets, nostalgia has, it would seem, become a trend in its own right.
有yǒu趣qù的de是shì，最zuì热衷rèzhōng于yú怀旧huáijiù的de，往往wǎngwǎng不bú是shì经历jīnglì过guò那个nàge年代niándài的de长辈zhǎngbèi，而是érshì从未cóngwèi亲身qīnshēn经历jīnglì过guò的de年轻人niánqīngrén。
Interestingly, those most keen on nostalgia are often not the elders who actually lived through that era, but young people who never personally experienced it at all.
心理学家xīnlǐxuéjiā指出zhǐchū，这zhè种zhǒng现象xiànxiàng并非bìngfēi对duì某mǒu个ge具体jùtǐ年代niándài的de向往xiàngwǎng，而是érshì对duì确定性quèdìngxìng的de渴望kěwàng。
Psychologists point out that this phenomenon is not really longing for a specific era at all, but rather a craving for certainty.
在zài一yí个ge变化biànhuà过guò快kuài、未来wèilái难以nányǐ预测yùcè的de时代shídài，一yí个ge被bèi简化jiǎnhuà、被bèi美化měihuà的de过去guòqù，显得xiǎnde格外géwài安全ānquán。
In an era of overly rapid change where the future is hard to predict, a simplified, idealized past appears especially safe.
换句话说huàn jù huà shuō，年轻人niánqīngrén怀念huáiniàn的de并非bìngfēi真实zhēnshí的de过去guòqù，而是érshì一yí个ge被bèi剪辑jiǎnjí过guò的de版本bǎnběn：只zhǐ留下liúxià温暖wēnnuǎn，剪掉jiǎndiào了le艰难jiānnán。
In other words, what young people are nostalgic for is not the real past, but an edited version — one that keeps only the warmth and cuts out the hardship.
值得zhídé注意zhùyì的de是shì，适度shìdù的de怀旧huáijiù并bìng非fēi坏事huàishì：研究yánjiū显示xiǎnshì，它tā能néng增强zēngqiáng归属感guīshǔgǎn，缓解huǎnjiě孤独gūdú情绪qíngxù。
Notably, moderate nostalgia is not actually a bad thing: research shows it can strengthen a sense of belonging and relieve loneliness.
然而rán'ér，一旦yídàn怀旧huáijiù变成biànchéng逃避táobì现实xiànshí的de借口jièkǒu，就jiù容易róngyì让ràng人rén陷入xiànrù一zhǒng“过去guòqù总zǒng比bǐ现在xiànzài好hǎo”的de幻觉huànjué。
However, once nostalgia becomes an excuse to escape reality, it easily traps people in the illusion that "the past was always better than the present."
真正zhēnzhèng健康jiànkāng的de怀旧huáijiù，应该yīnggāi是shì带着dàizhe过去guòqù的de温度wēndù，坦然tǎnrán地de走向zǒuxiàng未知wèizhī的de明天míngtiān。
Truly healthy nostalgia should carry the warmth of the past while calmly heading toward an unknown tomorrow.

Grammar note 语法点

并非…，而是…

A formal way to negate a common assumption before offering the real explanation: 'is not really A, but rather B'. Stronger and more literary than 不是…而是…. 例：他的沉默并非冷漠，而是深思。

这种现象并非对某个具体年代的向往，而是对确定性的渴望。 — This phenomenon is not really longing for a specific era, but rather a craving for certainty.
值得注意的是，…

A formal opener meaning 'it's worth noting that...', used to introduce a nuance or complication after making a point. 例：值得注意的是，压力并非总是坏事。

值得注意的是，适度的怀旧并非坏事：研究显示，它能增强归属感，缓解孤独情绪。 — Notably, moderate nostalgia is not actually a bad thing: research shows it can strengthen belonging and relieve loneliness.
一旦…，就…

'Once A happens, B follows' — introduces a turning point where something positive can tip into something problematic. 例：一旦失去信任，就很难重新建立。

一旦怀旧变成逃避现实的借口，就容易让人陷入“过去总比现在好”的幻觉。 — Once nostalgia becomes an excuse to escape reality, it easily traps people in the illusion that the past was always better.
