HSK 2 Grammar 基础语法

A 更 + adj（A is more..., used to persuade）

更 before an adjective can recommend one option over another by saying it's even more so.

羊肉更好吃。— The lamb is even more delicious.
Seen in 好吃吗菜单

A 比 B + adjective — comparison

比 compares two things: A 比 B 快 means 'A is faster than B'. Don't add 很 before the adjective in a 比 sentence.

今天的菜比饭馆的好吃。 — Today's food is tastier than the restaurant's.
Seen in 买手机便宜还是贵一起做饭羊肉还是鱼 +2

A 比 B 还 + adj（even more than）

比 compares A to B; adding 还 before the adjective says A goes even further than B.

你妈妈做的菜，比饭馆的还好吃。— Your mom's cooking is even better than a restaurant's.
Seen in 真好吃

A 离 B + 近/远 — distance between two places

离 marks the reference point when talking about distance: A 离 B 近/远 means 'A is close to/far from B.'

我家离图书馆很近。 — My home is very close to the library.
Seen in 离家很近图书馆旅游计划

A 离 B + 远/近（measuring distance between places）

离 measures the distance between two places: 我家离公司很近.

公园离我们家远不远？— Is the park far or not far from our house?
Seen in 问路多远

Adj + 多了 — much more (comparative change)

Placed after an adjective, 多了 means 'much more (adj)' — used for a big change compared with before.

我觉得好多了。 — I felt much better.
Seen in 累了就休息医院挂号

Adjective + 得 + result

得 after an adjective shows how far it goes: 热得不想说话 (so hot that ...).

我热得不想说话。 — I was so hot that I didn't want to talk.
Seen in 天气预报

V + 一下 — a bit; briefly

一下 after a verb shows the action is brief or casual.

在床上休息了一下。 — Rested on the bed for a bit.
Seen in 累了就休息

V + 了 + V — doing something briefly

Repeating a verb with 了 in between (聊了聊, 看了看) shows the action was brief or casual.

我们聊了聊公司的新项目。 — We chatted a bit about the company's new project.
Seen in 在酒店和同事吃午饭

V + 了 + 时间段（how long something has gone on）

了 after a verb, followed by a length of time, shows how long the action already lasted.

看了一个小时 — (they) read for an hour.
Seen in 回头见

V + 到 — reaching a destination

到 right after a verb like 寄, 走 or 送 marks the place the action reaches.

快递寄到北京。 — The package is sent to Beijing.
Seen in 寄快递

V + 吧（a friendly invitation）

Adding 吧 after a verb turns it into a warm invitation rather than a command.

你来吃吧！— Come and have some!
Seen in 你在做什么

V + 完 + 了 — finishing an action

完 right after a verb shows the action is finished; add 了 to mark it as a completed event.

剪完了头发，我很高兴。 — After finishing the haircut, I was happy.
Seen in 理发

V + 完 + 就 + V（as soon as ... then ...）

完 shows the first action is finished; 就 introduces what happens right after.

我做完就来。— I'll come right after I finish.
Seen in 等一下

V + 得 + adjective（complement describing how an action turned out）

得 after a verb introduces a comment on how the action is done, e.g. 说得很快 (speaks fast), 学得很好 (has learned well).

朋友已经在门口等我了，说我来得正好。— My friend was already waiting at the door, and said I'd arrived just in time.
Seen in 离家很近去机场新年快乐早上跑步 +5

V + 得 + 状态 (degree complement)

得 after a verb links it to a description of how the action goes.

今天我玩得非常高兴。
Seen in 点菜去朋友家做客

V + 懂 — understanding successfully

听懂 and 看懂 add 懂 as a result to show the listening or reading was actually understood.

花能听懂你说话吗？ — Can the flowers understand what you say?
Seen in 养花

V + 着 (an action continuing as a state)

着 after a verb shows a state that continues (放着 = is lying there) or how a second action is done (笑着说 = said with a smile).

银行的门关着，一个人也没有。— The bank's door was closed, and there wasn't a single person around.
Seen in 请帮帮我红色的裙子照顾生病的朋友中秋节的月亮 +5

V + 着 + V — doing one action by means of another

着 after a verb can describe the manner of doing something else: 煮着吃 = 'eat (it) by boiling it.'

鸡蛋可以煮着吃。— Eggs can be eaten boiled.
Seen in 喝牛奶吃鸡蛋

V + 起来（to stand up）

起来 after a verb like 站 shows upward movement, such as standing up.

我站了起来，说：“我先走了，再见！”— I stood up and said: "I'm heading out first, bye!"
Seen in 我要走了

adj + 一点儿（asking for a small change — neutral）

adj + 一点儿 asks for a small change and doesn't carry a complaint — the opposite feel from 有点儿.

可以便宜一点儿吗？— Can it be a little cheaper?
Seen in 有点儿便宜还是贵

adj + 一点儿，可以吗？（asking for a bit more/less）

Adding 一点儿 after an adjective and asking 可以吗 politely requests a small change, like a discount.

便宜一点儿可以吗？— Can it be a little cheaper?
Seen in 太贵了

adj + 了 (change of state)

了 after an adjective shows a new state has appeared: became tired.

她累了,想休息。
Seen in 在酒店

adjective + 极了 — extremely …

极了 comes after an adjective to mean 'extremely'. It replaces 很 or 非常, so never use them together.

菜都好吃极了。— The food was all incredibly delicious.
Seen in 快乐的一天真好吃难忘的生日爬山

measure word + noun（量词 + 名词）

A number needs a specific measure word before the noun — not just 个 for everything: 一本书 (a book), 一杯咖啡 (a cup of coffee), 一块蛋糕 (a piece of cake), 一件衣服 (a piece of clothing).

我要买一本书，不是一个书。 — I want to buy a book, not "yí gè shū."
Seen in 一个还是两个

sentence + 吧 (suggestion)

吧 at the end of a sentence turns it into a soft suggestion or invitation, e.g. 走吧 (let's go), 买吧 (go ahead and buy it).

你穿红色最好看，买吧！ — Red looks best on you — buy it!
Seen in 红色的裙子

time word + 见 — arranging to meet again

Put a time word like 中午 or 晚上 before 见 to say when you'll see someone again.

中午见！— See you at noon!
Seen in 早上好

verb + 一次 — counting times

次 counts how many times an action happens, and it goes after the verb: 洗一次 (wash one load), 去过两次 (been there twice).

这件衣服我洗了两次。 — I washed this shirt twice.
Seen in 洗衣服

verb + 完 (finish doing something)

完 attaches right after a verb to show the action is finished — 唱完 is 'finish singing', 说完 is 'finish speaking.'

吃完了，我对服务员说：“买单！” — When we finished eating, I said to the waiter: "The bill, please!"
Seen in 我累了点菜准备考试服务员，买单 +1

……是什么意思？（asking what a word means）

Put a word or phrase before 是什么意思 to ask what it means, instead of what someone intends.

挑食是什么意思？— What does 'tiāoshí' mean?
Seen in 你是什么意思

…以前 — before; ago

以前 comes AFTER the time word or the action, the opposite of English: 三年以前 (three years ago), 吃饭以前 (before eating).

睡觉以前我看书。 — I read before going to sleep.
Seen in 为什么学中文

…以后，就… — after …, right away

以后 follows the first action (听了以后 = after hearing), and 就 before the second verb shows it happened immediately.

他听了以后，就不看手机了。 — After hearing that, he stopped looking at his phone.
Seen in 别玩手机了银行开户

…的人 — the person who …

The description goes BEFORE 的 + noun: 卖西瓜的人 = the person who sells watermelons. English puts it after the noun, Chinese never does.

开车的人是我朋友。 — The person driving is my friend.
Seen in 卖西瓜的人

…，所以… — result

所以 (suǒyǐ) introduces the result of what was said before: '..., so ...', e.g. 天气不好，所以我们不去了.

因为下雨，所以等车的人很多。— Because it was raining, many people were waiting for the bus.
Seen in 送礼物坐公共汽车

一 changes tone before a measure word

一 is yì before a 1st/2nd/3rd-tone measure word (like 本, 杯) and yí before a 4th-tone one (like 个, 件, 块).

一杯咖啡 (yì bēi)、一个人 (yí gè).
Seen in 一个还是两个

一……就…… — As soon as... then...

"As soon as A happens, B happens." The action after 一 immediately triggers the action after 就, as in 我们一见面就笑了.

一追剧就忘了时间。— The moment they start binge-watching, they forget the time.
Seen in 已经十点了追剧遥远的朋友火锅与朋友 +3

一会儿 + 就 + verb（in no time, ...）

一会儿 followed by 就 shows something happens quickly, after only a short while.

我一会儿就吃完了。— I finished it in no time.
Seen in 再来一个

一会儿见 vs 回头见 vs 明天见

All three mean "see you," but the timing differs: 一会儿见 is very soon (minutes away), 回头见 is later the same day, and 明天见 is the next day.

一会儿见 — see you in a bit； 回头见 — see you later today； 明天见 — see you tomorrow。
Seen in 回头见

一样 / 不一样 — 'the same' / 'different'

一样 means 'the same'; adding 不 gives 不一样, 'different': 我们做的菜不一样。

我们做的菜不一样。— The dishes we make are different.
Seen in 妻子和丈夫

一边……一边……(doing two things at the same time)

一边 A 一边 B describes two actions happening at the same time, each 一边 followed by a verb, e.g. 他一边吃饭一边看电视.

我一边骑车，一边看街道旁边的树和花。 — I look at the trees and flowers along the street while I ride.
Seen in 看报纸加个微信小心刷牙洗脸 +15

上午/中午/晚上 — parts of the day

These words mark different times of day and pair with different greetings or plans.

中午一起吃饭，好吗？— Let's eat lunch together at noon, OK?
Seen in 早上好

上次 / 这次 / 下次

次 (cì) is the measure word for times or occasions: 上次 'last time', 这次 'this time', 下次 'next time', e.g. 下次再来.

上次是你给的，这次我来！ — Last time you paid; this time it's on me!
Seen in 服务员，买单

下次（next time）

下次 introduces a plan for a future occasion.

下次我们一起踢球。— Next time, let's play soccer together.
Seen in 我很开心

下雪了 — 了 for a new situation

了 after a weather verb announces a change: 下雪了 (it has started snowing), 下雨了 (it has started raining).

外面下雪了。— It's snowing outside (it started to snow).
Seen in 雪天

不 tone change before 4th tone — negation sandhi

不 is normally pronounced bù, but before a 4th-tone syllable it changes to bú for smoother speech: 不大 (bú dà), 不太 (bú tài).

我们的教室不大。— Our classroom isn't big.
Seen in 教室里

不会（don't know how — no learned skill）

不会 + verb says you never learned how to do something, like baking or cooking.

我不会做蛋糕，也不会做菜。— I don't know how to make cakes, and I don't know how to cook either.
Seen in 我不会

不用 — 'don't need to'

不用 + verb means there's no need to do something — different from 不能 ('cannot') or 不要 ('don't want to'): 不用坐车 = 'no need to take a car.'

我每天不用坐车。— Every day I don't need to take a car.
Seen in 离家很近我只看看

不用谢（no need to thank me）

不用谢 literally means "no need to thank" — another common way to brush off thanks.

我笑着说：“不用谢，明天见！”— I smile and say: "No need to thank me, see you tomorrow!"
Seen in 不客气

不知道 + 怎么 + V（not knowing how to do something）

不知道 followed by 怎么 and a verb expresses not knowing how to do something.

我站在门口，看着雨，不知道怎么回家。— I stood at the door, looking at the rain, not knowing how to get home.
Seen in 我忘了

不知道 + 疑问句

不知道 is followed directly by a question phrase like 在哪儿 or 是谁 — no 吗 needed.

我不知道图书馆在哪儿。 — I don't know where the library is.
Seen in 我不知道

不能（cannot — not able to, due to circumstance）

不能 is different from 不会: it says you're not able to do something right now, not that you lack the skill.

你不能只坐着。— You can't just sit there.
Seen in 我不会

为什么 — asking why

为什么 sits where the answer would sit, right before the verb: 你为什么学中文? There is no word-order change like in English.

你为什么不去？ — Why aren't you going?
Seen in 为什么学中文

也 + negative（also not...）

也 links a second negative statement to the first, showing another thing that also isn't the case.

我不会做蛋糕，也不会做菜。— I don't know how to make cakes, and I don't know how to cook either.
Seen in 我不会

也 + verb（me too）

也 goes right before the verb to agree with what someone just said.

我也想你。— I miss you too.
Seen in 我想你

也 + 是/V

也 goes right before 是 or a verb to mean 'also' or 'too'.

我也是新同学。 — I'm also a new student.
Seen in 我不知道

什么时候？（asking about a day or general time）

什么时候 asks broadly about when something happens — a day, a period, or 'someday'.

我们什么时候见面？— When shall we meet?
Seen in 什么时候

从...到...（from...to...）

Works for both time and place: 从九点到十一点 (from 9 to 11), 从北京到上海 (from Beijing to Shanghai).

从李小龙到成龙，这些演员让世界认识了中国功夫。— From Bruce Lee to Jackie Chan, these actors made the world know Chinese kung fu.
Seen in 我爱运动我累了卖西瓜的人功夫电影

从…开始 — starting from…

从 + time + 开始 marks when a new habit or plan begins: 从明天开始, 从今年开始.

从明天开始，我早一点儿起床！ — Starting tomorrow, I'll get up a little earlier!
Seen in 迟到了手表和时间

以后 — 'after ~'

Time word/verb phrase + 以后 means 'after ~': 放学以后 = 'after school lets out.'

放学以后，她喜欢和朋友一起做作业。— After school, she likes doing homework with friends.
Seen in 孩子上学

会 + 了（now you can do it）

会 means 'know how to'; adding 了 shows a change — you couldn't before, but now you can.

你现在会了！ — Now you've got it!
Seen in 一个还是两个

会 — a skill you have learned

会 means 'can' for learned skills like swimming or speaking a language: 我以前不会游泳，现在会了.

明天会很冷。 — Tomorrow it will be very cold.
Seen in 游泳天气预报

位（polite measure word for people）

位 is used to count people politely, especially by service staff, while 个 is the plain, everyday measure word.

我们三位。— Three of us.
Seen in 几位

你可以 + V

可以 here softly suggests an action — like saying 'you could' rather than asking permission.

你可以问李老师。 — You could ask Teacher Li.
Seen in 我不知道

你可以帮我 + V + 吗？

你可以 turns a request into a soft question — 吗 at the end makes it a clear question.

你可以帮我找车吗? — Could you help me find the train?
Seen in 请帮帮我

你在做什么？（what are you doing?）

在 here marks an ongoing action, so 你在做什么 asks what someone is doing right now.

你在做什么？— What are you doing?
Seen in 你在做什么

你是什么意思？（asking what someone means）

This asks about a person's intention behind what they said — are they joking, criticizing, or something else?

你是什么意思？你是说我不好吗？— What do you mean? Are you saying I'm bad?
Seen in 你是什么意思

你说什么？（asking someone to repeat what they said）

Use 你说什么？when you didn't catch the words themselves — different from 听不懂, which means you heard the words but didn't understand the meaning.

我问他：“你说什么？”— I ask him: "What did you say?"
Seen in 你说什么

先 + verb（to do something first）

先 before a verb shows that action happens before anything else.

弟弟先回房间了。— Younger brother went back to his room first.
Seen in 晚安

先（to do something first）

先 before a verb shows that action happens before anything else, such as arriving before someone else.

我先到了车站。— I arrived at the bus stop first.
Seen in 我到了

再 for something not yet done

再 before a verb tells someone to repeat or continue an action that hasn't happened yet.

你再往前走一点。— Walk a little further ahead.
Seen in 怎么走

再 vs 又（future repeat vs past repeat）

再 marks a repeated action that hasn't happened yet; 又 marks one that already did.

我等你再来。— I'll wait for you to come again (next time).
Seen in 太好了

再来 + 数量（bring/give me one more...）

再来 is a quick, colloquial way to ask for another one, often used when ordering food.

服务员，再来一碗！— One more bowl, please!
Seen in 再来一个

再要 + 数量，可以吗？（asking politely for one more）

再要 followed by a quantity, plus 可以吗, politely asks to get one more.

再要一碗，可以吗？— One more bowl, is that okay?
Seen in 再来一个

准备 + V（about to do something）

准备 before a verb often means someone is getting ready to do it, right before it happens.

我准备出门，朋友叫我。— I was about to go out when my friend called out to me.
Seen in 等一下

几位？（How many people?）

Waiters and staff use 几位 to politely ask how many people are in a group.

你们几位？— How many of you?
Seen in 几位

几点的 — "the one at what time"

的 turns a time into "the one at that time", so you can drop the noun: 九点的 = the nine o'clock one.

我们要九点的票。 — We want tickets for the nine o'clock.
Seen in 买火车票

别 + V + 了 — don't (do something)

别 before a verb tells someone not to do something.

别忘了带雨伞。 — Don't forget to bring an umbrella.
Seen in 看报纸

别 + verb (+ 了) — don't…

别 before a verb tells someone not to do something — 别担心 (don't worry) is one of the most common uses.

别担心，你已经很努力了。— Don't worry, you've already worked so hard.
Seen in 买火车票我怕别担心学做中国菜

别…了 — stop doing that

别 + verb + 了 asks somebody to STOP something they are already doing: 别玩手机了, 别说了. Without 了 it is a plain 'don't do it'.

别想了，快睡觉吧。 — Stop thinking about it and go to sleep.
Seen in 别玩手机了穿什么衣服

又 + verb（doing something again — already happened）

又 describes a repeat of an action that has already taken place, unlike 再 which is for something not yet done.

我念了一遍，又念了一遍，终于会读了。— I read it once, then again, and finally could read it.
Seen in 怎么说你说什么这个字怎么读

又 for something that already happened again

又 marks a repeated action that has already taken place, unlike 再 which marks one that hasn't happened yet.

我又给她打电话。— I called her again.
Seen in 我到了

又……又……(both ... and ...)

又…又… links two qualities or actions that are true at the same time. Both slots must be the same kind of word (two adjectives, or two verb phrases).

这种房子本身可能又小又旧，价格却贵得让人吃惊。 — The house itself may be both small and old, yet the price is shockingly high.
Seen in 打扫房间着急的一天难忘的生日锻炼身体 +8

又（again — for something already done）

又 marks a repeated action that has already happened, unlike 再 which is for an action not yet done.

我又去了附近的超市。— I went to the nearby supermarket again.
Seen in 有没有

只有 + amount — only have

只有 emphasizes a small or limited quantity.

我只有二十分钟时间，太着急了。 — I only had twenty minutes, and I was so anxious.
Seen in 已经十点了

只（only）

只 right before a verb narrows things down to just one option.

我只会说中文。— I can only speak Chinese.
Seen in 你会说英语吗

可以 + V — can; may

可以 expresses permission or possibility before a verb.

看报纸可以知道很多事情。 — Reading the newspaper lets you know about many things.
Seen in 看报纸

可以（can; may）

可以 offers permission or an option, often used when lending something to someone.

我有伞，你可以用我的。— I have an umbrella, you can use mine.
Seen in 我忘了

可能 + 会（might / probably will）

可能 right before 会 softens a prediction, useful for warning someone you might be late.

路上车很多，我可能会晚一点。— There's a lot of traffic on the way, I might be a little late.
Seen in 我马上到

吧（softening a suggestion）

吧 at the end of a sentence turns a statement into a suggestion, softer than a command.

穿那件红的吧。 — Why don't you wear that red one?
Seen in 走吧穿什么衣服

回 + place（to return to a place）

回 followed by a place means going back there.

我回自己的房间。— I go back to my own room.
Seen in 晚安

回头（to look back）

回头 describes turning your head to look behind you, often as you're walking away.

我走了，回头看，她还站在门口。— I started walking, then looked back — she was still standing at the door.
Seen in 我要走了

回来了（I'm back / you're back）

回来了 announces that someone has returned, and is also how you greet them when they walk in.

妈妈，我回来了！— Mom, I'm back!
Seen in 我回来了

因为…，所以… — cause and result

Chinese usually keeps BOTH halves of the pair, while English drops one of them. 因为 opens the reason, 所以 opens the result.

因为题很多，所以我每天晚上都准备两个小时。 — Because there are a lot of questions, I prepare for two hours every evening.
Seen in 洗衣服理发准备考试寄快递

在 + place + 下面 — under a place

在 + noun + 下面 tells you exactly where something is.

我在沙发下面找到了手机。 — I found the phone under the sofa.
Seen in 左边右边

在 + verb（doing something right now）

Put 在 before the verb for an action in progress: 他在看手机, 朋友在等我. 正在 means the same and is a little stronger.

我的朋友在等我。 — My friend is waiting for me.
Seen in 请进别玩手机了

地方 + 怎么走？(asking how to get somewhere)

Put a place before 怎么走 to ask for the route there.

图书馆怎么走？— How do I get to the library?
Seen in 怎么走

多 + verb + 一点儿（do a little more）

多 before a verb, with 一点儿 after it, means to do a bit more of that action.

你喜欢，就多吃一点儿。— If you like it, eat a little more.
Seen in 真好吃

如果……，就…… — if …, then …

如果 opens the condition. 就 belongs to the second clause and stands after its subject, right before the verb.

如果爬山那天下雨，我们就在城市里多玩一天。 — If it rains on the day of the climb, we'll spend one more day in the city.
Seen in 打扫房间和室友相处打算去旅行节约用水 +1

对 + noun + 好/不好 — 'good/bad for'

对 marks the target that something affects: X 对 Y 好 means 'X is good for Y.'

牛奶和鸡蛋对身体很好。— Milk and eggs are good for your health.
Seen in 喝牛奶吃鸡蛋

对 + someone + 说 — to say to someone

对 marks who the words are directed at — 对她说 means 'said to her', placed right before 说.

我对服务员说：“服务员，买单！” — I said to the waiter: "Waiter, the bill, please!"
Seen in 快乐的一天服务员，买单生日快乐

就 (sooner or easier than expected)

就 before the verb says something happened fast or earlier than you would think: 很快就好了 (it was ready in no time).

走路十分钟就到了。 — It's only a ten-minute walk and you're there.
Seen in 洗衣服图书馆

就 + adjective（a quick or direct reaction）

就 right before an adjective shows something happens right away, as a direct result.

我看见狗，就很着急。— As soon as I see a dog, I get nervous right away.
Seen in 我怕

左边 / 右边 + 有/没有 — describing location

左边 and 右边 mark position, often used with 有/没有 to say what's there.

桌子左边没有，你看看右边。 — It's not on the left side — look on the right.
Seen in 左边右边

已经...了 — 'already'

已经 goes before the verb and 了 closes the sentence. With a length of time it means the action started before and is still going on.

已经十点了，你怎么还没起床？ — It's already ten o'clock — why aren't you up yet?
Seen in 已经十点了过生日喝牛奶吃鸡蛋手表和时间 +1

希望 + clause (to hope that...)

希望 can be directly followed by a full clause describing what someone hopes will happen.

经理说，这个计划很重要，希望大家都努力做好。 — The manager said this plan was important, and hoped everyone would work hard to do it well.
Seen in 旅游计划开会

张 — measure word for flat things

Tickets, paper and tables take 张, never 个: 两张票, not 两个票.

两张票，一共六十块。 — Two tickets, sixty kuai in total.
Seen in 买火车票

往 + direction + verb

往 points a movement in a direction: 往前走 (walk ahead), 往左走 (go left).

到红绿灯往左走。— Turn left at the traffic light.
Seen in 问路

很高兴认识你/你们（nice to meet you）

很高兴认识你 is the standard line for meeting someone for the first time.

我很高兴认识你们。— I'm happy to meet you all.
Seen in 欢迎

忘了带（to forget to bring something）

忘了 followed by 带 and an object means you forgot to bring that thing along.

我忘了带伞。— I forgot to bring my umbrella.
Seen in 我忘了

快…了 — about to happen

快 + verb or adjective + 了 says something is on the point of happening: 鱼快好了 (the fish is nearly done), 我快到了 (I'm almost there).

饭快好了。 — Dinner is almost ready.
Seen in 一起做饭

怎么样？（how is it? asking for an opinion）

怎么样 asks for someone's general opinion or how something is — broader than 好吃吗, which asks specifically about taste.

朋友问我：“怎么样？”— My friend asked me: "How is it?"
Seen in 好吃吗还可以

怕 + noun（to be afraid of something）

怕 followed directly by a noun says you're afraid of that thing.

我怕狗。— I'm afraid of dogs.
Seen in 我怕

想 + person（to miss someone）

想 followed directly by a person means 'to miss' them; followed by a verb it means 'want to'.

我想你。— I miss you. / 你想吃什么？— What do you want to eat?
Seen in 我想你

想 + verb vs 要 + noun

想 before a verb is a softer, more tentative wish; 要 before a noun is a direct, concrete request.

我想买苹果，可是我要这个西瓜。— I wanted to buy apples, but I'll take this watermelon.
Seen in 我要这个

慢慢来（take your time）

慢慢 doubled before a verb tells someone not to rush — a common reply when a friend says they'll be late.

没关系，你慢慢来，我们等你。— It's OK, take your time, we'll wait for you.
Seen in 我马上到

我到了（I have arrived）

到了 after 我 tells someone you have just arrived — the most common line to say or text when meeting up.

我到了，你在哪儿？— I've arrived, where are you?
Seen in 我到了

我快到了，马上就到（I'm almost there, I'll be right there）

快到了 says you're nearly there, and 马上 emphasizes 'very soon' — the classic reassurance to send when someone is waiting for you.

我快到了，马上就到！— I'm almost there, I'll be right there!
Seen in 我马上到

我是说……（clarifying what you meant）

我是说 introduces a clarification, used to explain your real meaning after a misunderstanding.

不是，我是说你很有意思。— No, I mean you're really interesting.
Seen in 你是什么意思

我要走了 / 我先走了（I have to go / I'm heading out first）

Both phrases announce that you're about to leave; 先 adds that you're leaving before others.

时间不早了，我要走了。— It's getting late, I have to go.
Seen in 我要走了

打电话给 + person（to call someone）

给 after 打电话 introduces who is being called.

我打电话给妈妈。— I call mom.
Seen in 我想你

找不到 — cannot find (V + 不到 = unable to reach a result)

不到 after a verb shows the action fails to reach its goal.

我找不到我的手机了。 — I can't find my phone.
Seen in 左边右边

早上好 vs 早 — two ways to say good morning

早上好 is the full morning greeting; 早 alone is a shorter, casual way to say the same thing.

早！你也早！— Morning! Morning to you too!
Seen in 早上好

晚安（good night）

晚安 is said before going to sleep or leaving someone for the night.

晚安，明天见！— Good night, see you tomorrow!
Seen in 晚安

最 + adjective/verb (superlative)

最 means 'the most' and goes before an adjective or a verb like 喜欢, e.g. 最好, 最喜欢.

我最喜欢的是一本介绍咖啡的书。 — My favorite is a book about coffee.
Seen in 图书馆

有 + noun + 吗？（do you have...?）

Adding 吗 after 有 + noun is the more common, simpler way to ask if something is available.

这儿有雨伞吗？— Do you have umbrellas here?
Seen in 有没有

有人…,有人… — some people…, others…

Repeat 有人 to describe a lively scene: 有人跑步,有人唱歌 (some are jogging, some are singing).

公园里有人喝茶,有人说话。 — In the park some drink tea, some chat.
Seen in 周末去公园

有什么 + noun？（asking what there is）

有什么 followed by a noun asks what kinds of something are available.

你们有什么菜？— What dishes do you have?
Seen in 菜单

有多远？(asking exactly how far)

有多远 asks for the precise distance, once you already know it's 'far' in some sense.

我也不知道有多远。— I don't know exactly how far it is either.
Seen in 多远

有没有 + noun？（is there... or not?）

有没有 puts 有 and 没有 back to back to ask a yes/no question about whether something exists or is available, without needing 吗.

你们这儿有没有雨伞？— Do you have umbrellas here?
Seen in 有没有

有点儿 + adj（a little too... — usually a complaint）

有点儿 before an adjective means 'a little; somewhat', usually about something you are not happy with, e.g. 有点儿贵, 有点儿累.

箱子有点儿重，我搬的时候很小心。— The box was a bit heavy, and I carried it carefully.
Seen in 有点儿送礼物好吃吗红色的裙子 +2

有点儿 ≠ 一点儿

有点儿 sits before the adjective to complain (有点儿贵、有点儿累); 一点儿 sits after the adjective to ask for or describe a small change, and isn't negative (便宜一点儿、快一点儿).

我们打车回家吧，快一点儿。— Let's take a taxi home, a little faster.
Seen in 有点儿

次 — measure word for times

次 counts how many times something happens; the number goes before it: 两次 (twice), 每个星期去两次.

我们每个星期去游两次。— We go swimming twice every week.
Seen in 游泳

欢迎（welcome）

欢迎 greets someone arriving somewhere new, like a new coworker or a guest.

大家说：“欢迎你来我们公司！”— Everyone says: "Welcome to our company!"
Seen in 欢迎

正在 + V（right in the middle of doing something）

正在 emphasizes that an action is happening right now, even more clearly than 在 alone.

熊猫正在吃竹子，很可爱。 — The panda was eating bamboo — very cute.
Seen in 你在做什么动物园

每 … 都 …

每 (měi) 'every' is almost always paired with 都 (dōu) before the verb, e.g. 我每天都喝茶.

每年新年，我都回家看爸爸妈妈。 — Every New Year, I go home to visit my mom and dad.
Seen in 新年快乐踢足球

比 + 更（more ... than ...）

比 compares two things, and 更 adds emphasis to the difference.

位比个更客气。— Wèi is more polite than gè.
Seen in 几位

比 — comparison

A 比 B + adjective compares two things: 我比以前快. 一天比一天好 means better day by day.

我跑得比以前快。— I run faster than before.
Seen in 早上跑步

没…就… — did B without doing A

没 + verb A + 就 + verb B shows B happened right away, skipping A: 我没吃早饭就出门了.

我没吃早饭就出门了。— I left home without eating breakfast.
Seen in 迟到了

没事（表示不要紧：reassuring about a small mishap）

没事 answers a worried question like 你怎么了, telling someone nothing serious is wrong.

对不起，我忘了你的生日。没事，谢谢你打电话给我。— Sorry, I forgot your birthday. It's fine, thanks for calling.
Seen in 没事没事没事没关系 +1

没听清楚（didn't hear clearly）

清楚 means clear; 没听清楚 says the sound itself wasn't clear enough (too noisy, too far away) — not that the meaning was hard to understand.

太吵，我没听清楚。— It was too noisy, and I didn't hear clearly.
Seen in 你说什么

没问题 — 回应请求

没问题 means 'sure, I can do that' — use it to agree to a request or task.

你可以来吗? — 没问题! — Can you come? — Sure, no problem!
Seen in 没问题

看看 + noun（take a look at...）

看看, a doubled verb, means to take a quick look at something.

我看看菜单。— I take a look at the menu.
Seen in 菜单

看见 + person/thing（to catch sight of）

看见 marks the moment you notice someone or something.

我看见他，很高兴。— I see him and feel glad.
Seen in 我很开心

真的吗？（confirming surprising news）

Ask 真的吗 when you hear something hard to believe, to check whether it's really true.

真的吗？你怎么有两个手机？— Really? How come you have two phones?
Seen in 真的吗

真的（yes, really）

真的 alone answers 真的吗, confirming that something really is the case.

他说：真的，明天我就带来给你。— He says: Really, I'll bring it to you tomorrow.
Seen in 真的吗

祝 + person + wish

祝 (zhù) introduces a good wish for someone: 祝 + person + the thing you wish them, e.g. 祝你工作快乐.

祝你生日快乐！ — Happy birthday to you!
Seen in 过生日

第 + number — ordinal numbers

第 turns a number into an ordinal: 第一 (first), 第二 (second). 第一次 = the first time.

这是我第一次吃火锅。— This is my first time eating hotpot.
Seen in 第一次吃火锅

等 + person（to wait for someone）

等 directly followed by a person means you are waiting for them.

我等你。— I'll wait for you.
Seen in 太好了

等一下 / 等一会儿（wait a moment）

Both mean "wait a moment" and ask someone to hold on; 等一会儿 sounds a touch softer and slightly longer than 等一下.

等一下，我做完就来。— Wait a moment, I'll come as soon as I'm done.
Seen in 等一下

累不累 — the A-not-A question

Repeat the adjective or verb with 不 in the middle to make a yes/no question: 累不累? 是不是? 去不去? It means the same as adding 吗, and the 不 is unstressed.

你累不累？ — Are you tired?
Seen in 卖西瓜的人

累了就休息 — Adj/V + 了 + 就 + V

了 marks a change, and 就 introduces what happens next: "once X, then Y."

累了就应该休息一下。 — Once you're tired, you should rest a bit.
Seen in 累了就休息

给 + person + verb — do something for someone

给 puts the person who benefits before the verb: 给她买药 (buy medicine for her), 给他做饭 (cook for him).

我给她买了药和水果。— I bought medicine and fruit for her.
Seen in 帮助别人

虽然……但是……(although ... but ...)

虽然 may stand before or after the subject, and 但是 (or 可是) opens the second clause. Chinese keeps both halves, unlike English.

年轻人虽然更喜欢看别的电影，但是还是有很多人愿意再看老功夫片。— Although young people prefer other movies, many are still willing to watch old kung fu films again.
Seen in 买手机过生日唱歌跳舞等公共汽车 +10

要 + noun（I want ___, used to order or request something）

要 directly before a noun is how you ask for or point at something you want.

我要这个。— I want this one.
Seen in 我要这个

要…了 (about to happen)

要…了 says something is about to happen soon, e.g. 火车要开了 'the train is about to leave'.

他要来北京了。 — He is coming to Beijing soon.
Seen in 去机场新年快乐坐公共汽车

让 + someone + verb（to have/let someone do something）

让 means 'to have / ask someone to do something', introducing the person before the action.

我告诉她新地址，让她周末来我家玩。— I gave her my new address and told her to come visit on the weekend.
Seen in 生病了我搬家了网购退货银行开户

让 — to ask / let someone do something

让 + person + verb expresses letting or telling someone to do something: 大夫给我开了一些中药，让我每天喝一次。

妈妈让我早点儿回家。— Mom told me to come home early.
Seen in 帮助别人踢足球中医初体验无偿献血 +1

请 + V + 一点（please do a bit more...）

请 makes a request polite; adding 一点 after 慢 softens it into "a bit slower."

请说慢一点。— Please speak a little slower.
Seen in 请说慢一点

请 + 帮 + 我 + V

请帮我 + verb is a simple, polite way to ask someone to do something for you.

请帮我看看这个字。 — Please help me look at this character.
Seen in 请帮帮我

请再 + V + 一次（please do it again）

再...一次 asks someone to repeat an action one more time.

请再说一次。— Please say it again.
Seen in 请说慢一点

走 + 错（to go the wrong way）

错 right after a verb shows the action was done incorrectly.

我走错了，看见的是商店。— I went the wrong way; what I saw was a store.
Seen in 怎么走

走吧（let's go!）

走 means to leave, and adding 吧 turns it into a friendly suggestion — 'let's go!'

我们走吧！— Let's go!
Seen in 走吧

跑到 + place + 的时候（by the time you arrive）

到...的时候 marks the moment of arriving somewhere, showing what had already happened by then.

跑到电影院的时候已经晚了。— By the time I ran to the cinema, I was already late.
Seen in 没关系

跟 + person + verb — with someone

跟 introduces the person an action is done together with or toward.

她跟花说话。 — She talks to the flowers.
Seen in 养花

过了 + 时间（after a period of time）

过了 followed by a length of time shows how much time passed before the next thing happened.

过了一会儿，爸爸也下班回来了。— After a while, Dad also got off work and came home.
Seen in 我回来了

还 + V + 着（still doing something）

还...着 describes an action that is still going on.

小王还坐着。— Xiao Wang is still sitting there.
Seen in 走吧

还 + verb + 吗？（asking if something still holds）

还 before a verb checks whether an action is still going to happen or still true.

你还来图书馆吗？ — Are you still coming to the library?
Seen in 回头见

还可以（not bad; OK）

还可以 gives a neutral answer to 怎么样 — not great, not bad, right in the middle.

还可以，一般。— Not bad, average.
Seen in 还可以

还在 + verb — still doing

还 (still) + 在 (in the middle of) means an action is continuing: 雪还在下, 他还在睡觉.

晚上雪还在下。— In the evening it was still snowing.
Seen in 雪天

还是 vs 或者 — two kinds of 'or'

In QUESTIONS use 还是: 热的还是冰的? In statements use 或者: 我喝茶或者咖啡都可以.

你要茶还是咖啡? — Would you like tea or coffee?
Seen in 买咖啡

还没 + verb — not yet

还没 before a verb means something has still not happened: 车还没来 (the bus hasn't come yet).

我还没睡觉。 — I haven't gone to sleep yet.
Seen in 没问题等公共汽车

还行（pretty good — spoken version of 还可以）

还行 is the casual, spoken way to say something is not bad, often followed by a small complaint with 可是.

我也觉得还行，可是有点儿长。— I think it's not bad too, but it's a little long.
Seen in 还可以

这个 vs 那个（this vs that）

这个 points at something close by; 那个 points at something farther away.

这个太大了，那个小一点儿。— This one is too big, that one is a bit smaller.
Seen in 我要这个

这个用中文怎么说？（How do you say this in Chinese?）

This is the question to ask when you don't know a word — point at something and say this.

这个用中文怎么说？— How do you say this in Chinese?
Seen in 怎么说

进 vs 进来（to enter / to come in）

进 alone just means 'to enter'; adding 来 (进来) frames the movement as coming toward the speaker.

同学进来，看见我的桌子上有很多书。— My classmate comes in and sees lots of books on my table.
Seen in 请进

远不远？(yes/no question about distance)

Repeating 远 with 不 in between makes an A-not-A question asking simply whether something is far.

公园离我们家远不远？— Is the park far from our house or not?
Seen in 多远

送 + person + object

送 (sòng) takes two objects: first the person, then the thing given, e.g. 送他一本书 'give him a book (as a gift)'.

我想送她一件礼物。 — I want to give her a gift.
Seen in 送礼物

送 + person + thing（to give someone something）

送 puts the receiver right after the verb, then the gift — no 给 needed in between.

朋友说要送我一个新手机。— My friend says he wants to give me a new phone.
Seen in 真的吗

送 — to take/see someone somewhere

送 + person + place means to accompany or take someone there: 妈妈送她去学校 = 'Mom takes her to school.'

妈妈送她去学校。— Mom takes her to school.
Seen in 孩子上学

马上（right away）

马上 right before a verb says the action will happen very soon.

我马上到，你等一下。— I'll be there right away, wait a moment.
Seen in 什么时候

高兴 vs 开心（both mean happy, used differently）

高兴 usually reacts to one moment — seeing someone, hearing news. 开心 describes an overall mood, like how a whole day felt.

我看见他，很高兴。你今天开心吗？— I felt glad seeing him. Were you happy today (overall)?
Seen in 我很开心
