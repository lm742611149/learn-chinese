HSK 2 Grammar 基础语法

140 patterns, each with the reading it came from.

A + 比 + B + adjective

比 compares two things: A 比 B 好 means A is better than B.

小王跳得比我好。 — Xiao Wang dances better than me.
Seen in 唱歌跳舞
A 比 B + adj (comparison)

比 compares two things: A is more ... than B.

一百五十块,比白的便宜。
Seen in 便宜还是贵
A 比 B + adjective

比 compares two things: A 比 B 快 means 'A is faster than B'. Don't add 很 before the adjective in a 比 sentence.

它比我的旧手机快吗？ — Is it faster than my old phone?
Seen in 买手机羊肉还是鱼
A 比 B + adjective — comparison

比 puts two things side by side: A 比 B 好吃 = A is tastier than B. Do not add 很 before the adjective.

今天的菜比饭馆的好吃。 — Today's food is tastier than the restaurant's.
Seen in 一起做饭
A 离 B + 近/远

离 marks the distance between two places; put 近 (close) or 远 (far) after it, e.g. 我家离商店很远.

我家离图书馆很近。 — My home is very close to the library.
Seen in 图书馆
A 离 B + 近/远 (distance)

离 states the distance between A and B, followed by 近 (near) or 远 (far).

宾馆离火车站很近。
Seen in 旅游计划
A 离 B + 近/远 — distance between two places

离 marks the reference point when talking about distance: A 离 B 近/远 means 'A is close to/far from B.'

我的公司离我家很近。— My company is close to my home.
Seen in 离家很近
A 离 B + 远/近

离 measures the distance between two places: 我家离公司很近.

北京路离这儿远吗？— Is Beijing Road far from here?
Seen in 问路
A 还是 B？

还是 (háishi) links two options in an alternative question — 'A or B?'. Unlike English 'or', it is only used in questions, e.g. 你喝茶还是咖啡？

你们想吃羊肉还是鱼？ — Would you like to eat lamb or fish?
Seen in 羊肉还是鱼
Adj + 多了 — much more (comparative change)

多了 after an adjective shows a big improvement compared to before.

我觉得好多了。 — I felt much better.
Seen in 累了就休息
Adj + 极了 — extremely

极了 after an adjective intensifies it to mean "extremely."

我高兴极了。 — I was extremely happy.
Seen in 快乐的一天
Adjective + 得 + result

得 after an adjective shows how far it goes: 热得不想说话 (so hot that ...).

我热得不想说话。 — I was so hot that I didn't want to talk.
Seen in 天气预报
V + 一下 — a bit; briefly

一下 after a verb shows the action is brief or casual.

在床上休息了一下。 — Rested on the bed for a bit.
Seen in 累了就休息
V + 了 (completed action)

了 right after a verb marks the action as completed.

我买了一些苹果和西瓜送给他。
Seen in 去朋友家做客
V + 了 + V — doing something briefly

Repeating a verb with 了 in between (聊了聊, 看了看) shows the action was brief or casual.

我们聊了聊公司的新项目。 — We chatted a bit about the company's new project.
Seen in 和同事吃午饭
V + 到 — reaching a destination

到 right after a verb like 寄, 走 or 送 marks the place the action reaches.

快递寄到北京。 — The package is sent to Beijing.
Seen in 寄快递
V + 完 (finish doing)

完 after a verb shows the action is finished.

吃完饭,我们就回学校上课了。
Seen in 点菜
V + 完 + 了 — finishing an action

完 right after a verb shows the action is finished; add 了 to mark it as a completed event.

剪完了头发，我很高兴。 — After finishing the haircut, I was happy.
Seen in 理发
V + 完 + 就 + V（as soon as ... then ...）

完 shows the first action is finished; 就 introduces what happens right after.

我做完就来。— I'll come right after I finish.
Seen in 等一下
V + 得 + adjective — degree complement

得 links a verb to a description of how it's done or its degree: 住得很远 = 'lives far away.'

我的同事住得很远。— My coworker lives far away.
Seen in 离家很近
V + 得 + 状态 (degree complement)

得 links a verb to a description of how the action happens.

菜来得很快。
Seen in 点菜去朋友家做客
V + 懂 — understanding successfully

听懂 and 看懂 add 懂 as a result to show the listening or reading was actually understood.

花能听懂你说话吗？ — Can the flowers understand what you say?
Seen in 养花
V + 着

着 after a verb shows one action happening alongside another, like smiling while speaking.

老师笑着说。 — The teacher said, smiling.
Seen in 请帮帮我
V + 着 + V — doing one action by means of another

着 after a verb can describe the manner of doing something else: 煮着吃 = 'eat (it) by boiling it.'

鸡蛋可以煮着吃。— Eggs can be eaten boiled.
Seen in 喝牛奶吃鸡蛋
V 了 V (do briefly)

Repeating a verb with 了 in between means doing it briefly or casually.

看了看手机,就睡觉了。
Seen in 在酒店
Verb + 完 (+ 了)

完 (wán) after a verb means the action is finished, e.g. 做完 'finish doing', 吃完 'finish eating'.

吃完了，我对服务员说：“买单！” — When we finished eating, I said to the waiter: "The bill, please!"
Seen in 服务员，买单
Verb + 得 + adjective

得 links a verb to a word that describes how the action is done: 开得很慢.

我们开得很慢。 — We drove very slowly.
Seen in 去机场新年快乐
adj + 一点儿 (a little more...)

一点儿 after an adjective softens it: a bit cheaper, a bit bigger.

有便宜一点儿的吗?
Seen in 便宜还是贵
adj + 一点儿，可以吗？（asking for a bit more/less）

Adding 一点儿 after an adjective and asking 可以吗 politely requests a small change, like a discount.

便宜一点儿可以吗？— Can it be a little cheaper?
Seen in 太贵了
adj + 了 (change of state)

了 after an adjective shows a new state has appeared: became tired.

她累了,想休息。
Seen in 在酒店
sentence + 吧 (suggestion)

吧 at the end of a sentence turns it into a soft suggestion or invitation, e.g. 走吧 (let's go), 买吧 (go ahead and buy it).

你穿红色最好看，买吧！ — Red looks best on you — buy it!
Seen in 红色的裙子
verb + 一次 — counting times

次 counts how many times an action happens, and it goes after the verb: 洗一次 (wash one load), 去过两次 (been there twice).

这件衣服我洗了两次。 — I washed this shirt twice.
Seen in 洗衣服
verb + 完 (result: finished)

完 after a verb shows the action is completed, e.g. 吃完 (finish eating), 做完 (finish doing).

考完了我要睡十个小时！ — Once the exam is over, I'm going to sleep for ten hours!
Seen in 准备考试
verb + 得 + adjective

得 after a verb introduces a comment on how the action is done, e.g. 说得很快 (speaks fast), 学得很好 (has learned well).

你已经学得很好了。 — You've already learned it very well.
Seen in 准备考试
verb + 得 + adjective — degree complement

得 links a verb to how it is done: 跑得快 (run fast), 说得好 (speak well).

他跑得很快。— He runs very fast.
Seen in 早上跑步
verb + 着 (ongoing state)

着 after a verb marks a continuing state or the manner of another action, e.g. 穿着 (be wearing), 笑着说 (say while smiling).

今天我穿着新裙子去朋友家。 — Today I wore my new skirt to a friend's house.
Seen in 红色的裙子
……是什么意思？（asking what a word means）

Put a word or phrase before 是什么意思 to ask what it means, instead of what someone intends.

挑食是什么意思？— What does 'tiāoshí' mean?
Seen in 你是什么意思
…以前 — before; ago

以前 comes AFTER the time word or the action, the opposite of English: 三年以前 (three years ago), 吃饭以前 (before eating).

睡觉以前我看书。 — I read before going to sleep.
Seen in 为什么学中文
…以后，就… — after …, right away

以后 follows the first action (听了以后 = after hearing), and 就 before the second verb shows it happened immediately.

他听了以后，就不看手机了。 — After hearing that, he stopped looking at his phone.
Seen in 别玩手机了
…的人 — the person who …

The description goes BEFORE 的 + noun: 卖西瓜的人 = the person who sells watermelons. English puts it after the noun, Chinese never does.

开车的人是我朋友。 — The person driving is my friend.
Seen in 卖西瓜的人
…的时候 (when…)

Put 的时候 after a phrase to mean 'when / by the time that…', e.g. 吃饭的时候 (while eating).

他到公司的时候，大家都开始工作了。 — By the time he got to the office, everyone had started working.
Seen in 手表和时间
…的时候 — when; while

Put the whole action in front of 的时候: 我做菜的时候 = while I was cooking. The main clause tells you whether it is past or present.

我们吃饭的时候，她说了很多话。 — While we were eating, she talked a lot.
Seen in 一起做饭
…，所以…

所以 (suǒyǐ) introduces the result of what was said before: '..., so ...', e.g. 天气不好，所以我们不去了.

我不知道买什么，所以问了姐姐。 — I didn't know what to buy, so I asked my older sister.
Seen in 送礼物
…，所以… — result

所以 introduces the result of a reason: 公司离家很远，所以我坐公共汽车. Often paired with 因为.

因为下雨，所以等车的人很多。— Because it was raining, many people were waiting for the bus.
Seen in 坐公共汽车
一…就… — as soon as…, then…

一 + V1，就 + V2 shows the second action happens right after the first.

我一听，马上就起床了。 — As soon as I heard that, I got up right away.
Seen in 已经十点了
一样 / 不一样 — 'the same' / 'different'

一样 means 'the same'; adding 不 gives 不一样, 'different': 我们做的菜不一样。

我们做的菜不一样。— The dishes we make are different.
Seen in 妻子和丈夫
一起 + V — to do something together

一起 before a verb shows two or more people doing the action together.

我们一起唱歌。 — We sang together.
Seen in 快乐的一天
一边…，一边… — doing two things at once

一边 + V1，一边 + V2 describes two actions happening at the same time.

他一边喝茶，一边看报纸。 — He drinks tea while reading the newspaper.
Seen in 看报纸
上次 / 这次 / 下次

次 (cì) is the measure word for times or occasions: 上次 'last time', 这次 'this time', 下次 'next time', e.g. 下次再来.

上次是你给的，这次我来！ — Last time you paid; this time it's on me!
Seen in 服务员，买单
下雪了 — 了 for a new situation

了 after a weather verb announces a change: 下雪了 (it has started snowing), 下雨了 (it has started raining).

外面下雪了。— It's snowing outside (it started to snow).
Seen in 雪天
不 tone change before 4th tone — negation sandhi

不 is normally pronounced bù, but before a 4th-tone syllable it changes to bú for smoother speech: 不大 (bú dà), 不太 (bú tài).

我们的教室不大。— Our classroom isn't big.
Seen in 教室里
不用 — 'don't need to'

不用 + verb means there's no need to do something — different from 不能 ('cannot') or 不要 ('don't want to'): 不用坐车 = 'no need to take a car.'

我每天不用坐车。— Every day I don't need to take a car.
Seen in 离家很近
不知道 + 疑问句

不知道 is followed directly by a question phrase like 在哪儿 or 是谁 — no 吗 needed.

我不知道图书馆在哪儿。 — I don't know where the library is.
Seen in 我不知道
为什么 — asking why

为什么 sits where the answer would sit, right before the verb: 你为什么学中文? There is no word-order change like in English.

你为什么不去？ — Why aren't you going?
Seen in 为什么学中文
也 + 是/V

也 goes right before 是 or a verb to mean 'also' or 'too'.

我也是新同学。 — I'm also a new student.
Seen in 我不知道
了 — change of state

Sentence-final 了 marks a new situation: 生病了 (got sick — wasn't before), 好多了 (much better now than before).

我现在好多了。— I feel much better now.
Seen in 生病了
了 — completed action / new situation

了 placed after a verb marks that an action has happened or a new state has come about: 她认识了很多新朋友 = 'She has come to know many new friends.'

她认识了很多新朋友。— She has made many new friends.
Seen in 孩子上学
从 A 到 B — from A to B

Works for both time and place: 从九点到十一点 (from 9 to 11), 从北京到上海 (from Beijing to Shanghai).

我们从九点玩儿到十一点。 — We play from nine to eleven.
Seen in 我爱运动
从…到… — from … to …

从 marks the starting point and 到 the end point, for both time and place: 从早上到晚上, 从北京到上海.

他从早上八点卖到晚上七点。 — He sells from eight in the morning until seven at night.
Seen in 卖西瓜的人
从…开始

从 + time + 开始 means 'starting from…', e.g. 从今天开始 (starting today).

从明天开始，我早一点儿起床！ — Starting tomorrow, I'll get up a little earlier!
Seen in 手表和时间
从…开始 — starting from…

从 + time + 开始 marks when a new habit or plan begins: 从明天开始, 从今年开始.

从明天开始，我要早点儿起床。— Starting tomorrow, I'll get up a bit earlier.
Seen in 迟到了
以后 — 'after ~'

Time word/verb phrase + 以后 means 'after ~': 放学以后 = 'after school lets out.'

放学以后，她喜欢和朋友一起做作业。— After school, she likes doing homework with friends.
Seen in 孩子上学
会 (prediction)

会 before an adjective or verb can mean "will (probably)": 明天会很冷。

明天会很冷。 — Tomorrow it will be very cold.
Seen in 天气预报
会 — a skill you have learned

会 means 'can' for learned skills like swimming or speaking a language: 我以前不会游泳，现在会了.

我以前不会游泳。— I couldn't swim before.
Seen in 游泳
你可以 + V

可以 here softly suggests an action — like saying 'you could' rather than asking permission.

你可以问李老师。 — You could ask Teacher Li.
Seen in 我不知道
你可以帮我 + V + 吗？

你可以 turns a request into a soft question — 吗 at the end makes it a clear question.

你可以帮我找车吗? — Could you help me find the train?
Seen in 请帮帮我
你是什么意思？（asking what someone means）

This asks about a person's intention behind what they said — are they joking, criticizing, or something else?

你是什么意思？你是说我不好吗？— What do you mean? Are you saying I'm bad?
Seen in 你是什么意思
准备 + V（about to do something）

准备 before a verb often means someone is getting ready to do it, right before it happens.

我准备出门，朋友叫我。— I was about to go out when my friend called out to me.
Seen in 等一下
别 + V + 了 — don't (do something)

别 before a verb tells someone not to do something.

别忘了带雨伞。 — Don't forget to bring an umbrella.
Seen in 看报纸
别…了

别 + verb + 了 tells someone to stop doing something: 别想了 (stop thinking).

别想了，快睡觉吧。 — Stop thinking about it and go to sleep.
Seen in 穿什么衣服
别…了 — stop doing that

别 + verb + 了 asks somebody to STOP something they are already doing: 别玩手机了, 别说了. Without 了 it is a plain 'don't do it'.

别玩手机了，快吃吧。 — Put the phone down and eat.
Seen in 别玩手机了
又…又… — both...and...

又 + adjective + 又 + adjective describes two qualities happening at the same time.

我觉得又累又饿。 — I feel both tired and hungry.
Seen in 打扫房间
只有 + amount — only have

只有 emphasizes a small or limited quantity.

我只有二十分钟时间，太着急了。 — I only had twenty minutes, and I was so anxious.
Seen in 已经十点了
可以 + V — can; may

可以 expresses permission or possibility before a verb.

看报纸可以知道很多事情。 — Reading the newspaper lets you know about many things.
Seen in 看报纸
吧 (suggestion)

吧 at the end of a sentence makes a soft suggestion: 穿那件红的吧。

穿那件红的吧。 — Why don't you wear that red one?
Seen in 穿什么衣服
听不懂（can't understand by listening）

听不懂 means you heard the words but the meaning didn't get through — different from just not hearing at all.

我听不懂，请说慢一点。— I don't understand; please speak a little slower.
Seen in 请说慢一点
因为…，所以…

This pair links a reason and its result. Unlike English, Chinese often uses both words together, e.g. 因为下雨，所以我不去.

因为题很多，所以我每天晚上都准备两个小时。 — Because there are a lot of questions, I prepare for two hours every evening.
Seen in 准备考试
因为…，所以… — because …, so …

Chinese usually keeps BOTH halves of the pair, while English drops one of them. 因为 opens the reason, 所以 opens the result.

因为下雨了，所以我们没出去。 — Because it rained, we didn't go out.
Seen in 洗衣服
因为…，所以… — because, so

因为 introduces the reason; in casual speech the result can also follow directly without 所以.

因为夏天太热了，我想剪短一点。 — Because summer is too hot, I want to cut it shorter.
Seen in 理发
因为…，所以… — cause and result

因为 gives the reason and 所以 gives the result; both can appear together in the same sentence.

因为东西不重，所以只要二十块钱。 — Because the item isn't heavy, it only costs twenty kuai.
Seen in 寄快递
在 + place + 下面 — under a place

在 + noun + 下面 tells you exactly where something is.

我在沙发下面找到了手机。 — I found the phone under the sofa.
Seen in 左边右边
在 + place — location marker

在 marks where something or someone is located, usually placed right before the place word: 老师站在黑板前面 = 'the teacher stands in front of the blackboard.'

老师站在黑板前面。— The teacher stands in front of the blackboard.
Seen in 教室里
在 + verb — happening right now

Put 在 before the verb for an action in progress: 他在看手机, 朋友在等我. 正在 means the same and is a little stronger.

我的朋友在等我。 — My friend is waiting for me.
Seen in 别玩手机了
多少钱？（asking the price）

多少钱 is the standard way to ask how much something costs.

这个西瓜多少钱？— How much is this watermelon?
Seen in 太贵了
太 + adj + 了（too ...）

太...了 says something is excessively so, often with feeling.

太贵了！— Too expensive!
Seen in 太贵了
如果…就… — if...then...

如果 introduces a condition, and 就 introduces the result that follows.

如果每个星期都打扫，房间就不会那么乱了。 — If you clean every week, the room won't get so messy.
Seen in 打扫房间
对 + noun + 好/不好 — 'good/bad for'

对 marks the target that something affects: X 对 Y 好 means 'X is good for Y.'

牛奶和鸡蛋对身体很好。— Milk and eggs are good for your health.
Seen in 喝牛奶吃鸡蛋
对 + person + 说

对 (duì) marks the person you speak to: 对某人说 'say to someone', e.g. 老师对我说.

我对服务员说：“服务员，买单！” — I said to the waiter: "Waiter, the bill, please!"
Seen in 服务员，买单
对 + someone + 说 — to say to someone

对 introduces the person being spoken to before 说.

家人都对我说：“生日快乐！” — My family all said to me, "Happy birthday!"
Seen in 快乐的一天
就 (sooner or easier than expected)

就 before a verb signals that something happens quickly or easily, e.g. 五分钟就好了 (ready in just five minutes).

走路十分钟就到了。 — It's only a ten-minute walk and you're there.
Seen in 图书馆
就 — sooner than expected

就 before the verb says something happened fast or earlier than you would think: 很快就好了 (it was ready in no time).

衣服很快就好了。 — The clothes were ready in no time.
Seen in 洗衣服
左边 / 右边 + 有/没有 — describing location

左边 and 右边 mark position, often used with 有/没有 to say what's there.

桌子左边没有，你看看右边。 — It's not on the left side — look on the right.
Seen in 左边右边
已经 … 了

已经 (yǐjīng) with 了 at the end means something has 'already' happened or is already the case, e.g. 他已经走了.

我已经三十岁了。 — I'm already thirty years old.
Seen in 过生日
已经...了 — 'already'

已经 + verb (+ 了) shows something has already happened or become true: 已经习惯了 = 'already used to it.'

现在我每天都喝，已经习惯了。— Now I drink it every day; I'm already used to it.
Seen in 喝牛奶吃鸡蛋
已经…了 (already)

已经 pairs with sentence-final 了 to say something has already happened or is already the case.

时间已经是七点十分了。 — It was already ten past seven.
Seen in 手表和时间
已经…了 — already

已经 before a verb or time expression, with 了 at the end, shows something has already happened.

已经十点了，你怎么还没起床？ — It's already ten o'clock — why aren't you up yet?
Seen in 已经十点了为什么学中文
希望 + clause

希望 introduces a whole sentence expressing what you hope for.

我希望天气晴,我们能玩得很快乐。
Seen in 旅游计划
往 + direction + verb

往 points a movement in a direction: 往前走 (walk ahead), 往左走 (go left).

到红绿灯往左走。— Turn left at the traffic light.
Seen in 问路
快…了 — about to happen

快 + verb or adjective + 了 says something is on the point of happening: 鱼快好了 (the fish is nearly done), 我快到了 (I'm almost there).

饭快好了。 — Dinner is almost ready.
Seen in 一起做饭
我是说……（clarifying what you meant）

我是说 introduces a clarification, used to explain your real meaning after a misunderstanding.

不是，我是说你很有意思。— No, I mean you're really interesting.
Seen in 你是什么意思
找不到 — cannot find (V + 不到 = unable to reach a result)

不到 after a verb shows the action fails to reach its goal.

我找不到我的手机了。 — I can't find my phone.
Seen in 左边右边
是…的 (emphasizing time/place/manner of a past event)

Use 是…的 to highlight when, where, or how something already happened, e.g. 我是坐飞机来的 (I came by plane).

我的手机是五年前买的。 — I bought my phone five years ago.
Seen in 买手机
最 + adjective/verb (superlative)

最 means 'the most' and goes before an adjective or a verb like 喜欢, e.g. 最好, 最喜欢.

我最喜欢的是一本介绍咖啡的书。 — My favorite is a book about coffee.
Seen in 图书馆
有人…,有人… — some people…, others…

Repeat 有人 to describe a lively scene: 有人跑步,有人唱歌 (some are jogging, some are singing).

公园里有人喝茶,有人说话。 — In the park some drink tea, some chat.
Seen in 周末去公园
有点儿 + adjective

有点儿 (yǒudiǎnr) before an adjective means 'a bit (too) ...', usually about something not ideal, e.g. 有点儿贵.

衣服有点儿大。 — The clothes are a bit big.
Seen in 送礼物红色的裙子
次 — measure word for times

次 counts how many times something happens; the number goes before it: 两次 (twice), 每个星期去两次.

我们每个星期去游两次。— We go swimming twice every week.
Seen in 游泳
每 … 都 …

每 (měi) 'every' is almost always paired with 都 (dōu) before the verb, e.g. 我每天都喝茶.

每年新年，我都回家看爸爸妈妈。 — Every New Year, I go home to visit my mom and dad.
Seen in 新年快乐
每…都…

每 (every) is usually followed by 都 before the verb: 每个星期日都去。

他每个星期日都去踢足球。 — He goes to play football every Sunday.
Seen in 踢足球
比 — comparison

A 比 B + adjective compares two things: 我比以前快. 一天比一天好 means better day by day.

我跑得比以前快。— I run faster than before.
Seen in 早上跑步
没…就… — did B without doing A

没 + verb A + 就 + verb B shows B happened right away, skipping A: 我没吃早饭就出门了.

我没吃早饭就出门了。— I left home without eating breakfast.
Seen in 迟到了
没关系 — 回应道歉

没关系 means 'it's fine / never mind' — use it after someone says 对不起。

对不起! — 没关系。 — Sorry! — It's fine.
Seen in 没问题
没问题 — 回应请求

没问题 means 'sure, I can do that' — use it to agree to a request or task.

你可以来吗? — 没问题! — Can you come? — Sure, no problem!
Seen in 没问题
的时候 — 'when ~'

Verb/clause + 的时候 means 'when ~ happens': 下课的时候 = 'when class is over.'

下课的时候，同学们喜欢说话。— When class is over, my classmates like to talk.
Seen in 教室里妻子和丈夫
祝 + person + wish

祝 (zhù) introduces a good wish for someone: 祝 + person + the thing you wish them, e.g. 祝你工作快乐.

祝你生日快乐！ — Happy birthday to you!
Seen in 过生日
第 + number — ordinal numbers

第 turns a number into an ordinal: 第一 (first), 第二 (second). 第一次 = the first time.

这是我第一次吃火锅。— This is my first time eating hotpot.
Seen in 第一次吃火锅
等一下 / 等一会儿（wait a moment）

Both mean "wait a moment" and ask someone to hold on; 等一会儿 sounds a touch softer and slightly longer than 等一下.

等一下，我做完就来。— Wait a moment, I'll come as soon as I'm done.
Seen in 等一下
累不累 — the A-not-A question

Repeat the adjective or verb with 不 in the middle to make a yes/no question: 累不累? 是不是? 去不去? It means the same as adding 吗, and the 不 is unstressed.

你累不累？ — Are you tired?
Seen in 卖西瓜的人
累了就休息 — Adj/V + 了 + 就 + V

了 marks a change, and 就 introduces what happens next: "once X, then Y."

累了就应该休息一下。 — Once you're tired, you should rest a bit.
Seen in 累了就休息
给 + person + verb — do something for someone

给 puts the person who benefits before the verb: 给她买药 (buy medicine for her), 给他做饭 (cook for him).

我给她买了药和水果。— I bought medicine and fruit for her.
Seen in 帮助别人
虽然…但是…

"Although ... but ..." — both halves are used together in Chinese: 虽然我唱得不好，但是我很快乐。

虽然我唱得不好，但是我唱得很快乐。 — Although I don't sing well, I sing very happily.
Seen in 唱歌跳舞
虽然…但是… — although… but…

虽然 starts the first clause and 但是 starts the second; unlike English, both words appear together.

虽然等了很长时间，但是我很高兴。— Although I waited a long time, I was happy.
Seen in 等公共汽车
虽然…，但是…

This pair means 'although… , (but)…'. Chinese keeps both words in the sentence, e.g. 虽然很贵，但是很好.

虽然我的钱少了，但是我非常高兴！ — Although I have less money now, I'm really happy!
Seen in 买手机过生日
要 … 了

要…了 says something is about to happen soon, e.g. 火车要开了 'the train is about to leave'.

新年要到了。 — The New Year is almost here.
Seen in 新年快乐
要…了 (about to happen)

要 + verb + 了 says something is going to happen soon: 他要来北京了。

他要来北京了。 — He is coming to Beijing soon.
Seen in 去机场
要…了 — about to happen

要 + verb + 了 means something is about to happen very soon.

我要下车了。— I'm about to get off.
Seen in 坐公共汽车
让 (to ask / let someone do something)

Person A + 让 + person B + verb: A asks or lets B do something. 他让我去看。

他让我也去看。 — He asked me to go and watch too.
Seen in 踢足球
让 + person + verb

让 means to have or tell someone to do something: 医生让我多休息.

医生让我多喝水。— The doctor told me to drink more water.
Seen in 生病了
让 — to ask / let someone do something

让 + person + verb means to have or ask someone to do something: 她让我帮助她.

她让我帮助她。— She asked me to help her.
Seen in 帮助别人
请 + V + 一点（please do a bit more...）

请 makes a request polite; adding 一点 after 慢 softens it into "a bit slower."

请说慢一点。— Please speak a little slower.
Seen in 请说慢一点
请 + 帮 + 我 + V

请帮我 + verb is a simple, polite way to ask someone to do something for you.

请帮我看看这个字。 — Please help me look at this character.
Seen in 请帮帮我
请再 + V + 一次（please do it again）

再...一次 asks someone to repeat an action one more time.

请再说一次。— Please say it again.
Seen in 请说慢一点
跟 + person + verb — with someone

跟 introduces the person an action is done together with or toward.

她跟花说话。 — She talks to the flowers.
Seen in 养花
还在 + verb — still doing

还 (still) + 在 (in the middle of) means an action is continuing: 雪还在下, 他还在睡觉.

晚上雪还在下。— In the evening it was still snowing.
Seen in 雪天
还是 vs 或者 — two kinds of 'or'

In QUESTIONS use 还是: 热的还是冰的? In statements use 或者: 我喝茶或者咖啡都可以.

你要茶还是咖啡? — Would you like tea or coffee?
Seen in 买咖啡
还是 — 'or' in a question

还是 links two choices inside a question, e.g. 你想吃米饭还是面条？

你想吃米饭还是面条？ — Would you like rice or noodles?
Seen in 和同事吃午饭
还没 + V

还没 before a verb says the action hasn't happened yet.

我还没睡觉。 — I haven't gone to sleep yet.
Seen in 没问题
还没 + verb — not yet

还没 before a verb means something has still not happened: 车还没来 (the bus hasn't come yet).

公共汽车还没来。— The bus still hasn't come.
Seen in 等公共汽车
送 + person + object

送 (sòng) takes two objects: first the person, then the thing given, e.g. 送他一本书 'give him a book (as a gift)'.

我想送她一件礼物。 — I want to give her a gift.
Seen in 送礼物
送 — to take/see someone somewhere

送 + person + place means to accompany or take someone there: 妈妈送她去学校 = 'Mom takes her to school.'

妈妈送她去学校。— Mom takes her to school.
Seen in 孩子上学
都 — 'both/all' with a plural subject

都 follows a plural subject like 我们 or 他们 to mean 'both/all': 我们都很忙 = 'we are both busy.'

我们工作都很忙，但是我们都很高兴。— We're both busy with work, but we're both happy.
Seen in 妻子和丈夫
