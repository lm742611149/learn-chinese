A 和 B

和 joins two nouns, like English 'and': 我和朋友 = my friend and I. It does not join whole sentences.

中午我和朋友去饭馆吃饭。 — At noon my friend and I go to a restaurant to eat.
Seen in 星期天
A 和 B + verb

和 links two people doing something together: 'A does something with B'.

下午我和妈妈去商店。
Seen in 去商店
A 是 B

是 links two nouns to say A is B, like an equals sign: 今天是星期天 = today is Sunday.

今天是星期天。 — Today is Sunday.
Seen in 星期天
A 是 B — but never 是 + adjective

是 links two nouns: 我是学生 (I am a student). With adjectives use 很 instead: say 我很高兴, NOT 我是高兴.

他是老师,他很忙。 — He is a teacher; he is busy.
Seen in 自我介绍
A 的 B — possession with 的

的 links an owner to a thing: 饭馆的人 = the restaurant's staff, 妈妈的菜 = mom's cooking.

这是我的杯子。 — This is my cup.
Seen in 在饭馆
A 还是 B — 'or' in questions

还是 offers a choice between two options and makes the sentence a question by itself — no 吗 needed: 你喝茶还是喝咖啡？

你去还是不去？— Are you going or not?
Seen in 喝茶还是咖啡
Measure words 本 and 块

Chinese counts nouns with a measure word: 一本书 (one book), 二十块钱 (twenty yuan). 一 is read yì before a third-tone syllable like běn.

一本书二十块钱。 — One book costs twenty yuan.
Seen in 我的钱
Noun + 里 / 上 — location words

Attach 里 (inside) or 上 (on top) after a noun to make a place: 房间里 (in the room), 桌子上 (on the desk). Then use 有 for what is there.

桌子上有电脑。 — There is a computer on the desk.
Seen in 我的房间
Number + 点 — telling the time

点 marks the hour. Put the time BEFORE the verb: 我七点起床 (I get up at seven). Never say 我起床七点.

我十一点睡觉。 — I go to bed at eleven.
Seen in 我的一天
Place + 里/前面 + 有 — there is / there are

To say something exists at a place, put the place word first, then 有: 学校里有很多同学。学校前面有一个商店。

学校前面有一个小商店。 — There is a small shop in front of the school.
Seen in 我的学校
Statement + 吗

Add 吗 to the end of a statement to turn it into a yes-no question.

你想来北京吗？ — Would you like to come to Beijing?
Seen in 我住在哪儿
Time before the verb

Time words like 早上七点 come before the verb, and usually right after the subject.

他早上七点去商店。 — He goes to the store at seven in the morning.
Seen in 爸爸工作
Time words before the verb

Chinese verbs do not change for tense. Time words like 昨天 / 今天 / 明天 at the start of the sentence tell you when: 昨天下雨。明天我不去学校。

明天我不去学校。 — Tomorrow I won't go to school.
Seen in 昨天今天明天
Tone change of 不 (bù → bú)

不 is normally bù, but before a 4th-tone syllable it becomes bú: 不去 (bú qù), 不下雨 (bú xiàyǔ).

我不去学校。 — I am not going to school.
Seen in 昨天今天明天
X多少钱? — asking the price of something

To ask how much something costs, put the noun before 多少钱: 衣服多少钱? = How much are the clothes?

这些衣服多少钱？ — How much are these clothes?
Seen in 多少钱
X多少钱一个? — asking the price per item

Add 一个 (or another measure word) after 多少钱 to ask the price of just one item: 杯子多少钱一个? = How much is one cup?

杯子多少钱一个？ — How much is one cup?
Seen in 多少钱
number + 月 + number + 号 — giving a date

Chinese goes big to small: month first, then day. 九月五号 = September 5th. In writing you may also see 日 instead of 号.

今天是九月五号。 — Today is September 5th.
Seen in 几月几号
person + number + 岁

Ages need no verb in Chinese — just put the number and 岁 right after the person: 我三十岁 = I am thirty.

我女儿三岁。 — My daughter is three years old.
Seen in 数字真有趣
person + 好 — greeting a specific person

Put 好 right after a title or name to greet that person, like 老师好 ("hello, teacher").

老师好！ — Hello, teacher!
Seen in 你好
sentence + 了

了 at the end of a sentence signals that something has happened or a new situation has arrived: 到北京了 = we've arrived in Beijing.

飞机到北京了。 — The plane has arrived in Beijing.
Seen in 在飞机上
sentence + 吗？

Add 吗 to the end of a statement to turn it into a yes/no question — the word order stays the same.

你会写汉语的数字吗？ — Can you write numbers in Chinese?
Seen in 数字真有趣
verb + 了

了 right after a verb marks the action as completed.

我们买了六个苹果和一些水。
Seen in 去商店
verb + 了 (completed action)

了 right after a verb shows the action is finished, often with an amount after it.

我喝了三杯水。 — I drank three cups of water.
Seen in 太热了
verb + 了 — completed action

了 right after a verb shows the action is finished or has happened: 买了 = bought.

我买了五个小苹果。 — I bought five small apples.
Seen in 买水果
verb + 的 + noun — a modifier before a noun

A whole phrase can describe a noun if you add 的: 我写的字 = the characters that I write.

我写的字不太好。 — The characters I write are not very good.
Seen in 在学校学习
verb phrase + 的 + noun

A short phrase plus 的 can describe a noun: 妈妈做的菜 = the food that Mom makes.

我爱妈妈做的菜。 — I love the food my mom makes.
Seen in 妈妈做的菜
……的时候 — when / while …

Put 的时候 after an action to say "while doing it": 看电影的时候 (while watching the movie).

看电影的时候，我们喝茶。 — While watching the movie, we drank tea.
Seen in 看电影
……，好吗？ — asking "okay?"

Add 好吗 after a suggestion to gently ask for agreement: 星期天回家吃饭，好吗？

星期天回家吃饭，好吗？ — Come home for a meal on Sunday, okay?
Seen in 打电话
…了 (change of state)

了 at the end of a sentence signals that a situation has changed — something new is happening now.

中午下雨了。 — At noon it started to rain.
Seen in 下雨了
…了 — a new situation

了 at the end of the sentence signals a change of state: 豆豆两岁了 = Doudou has turned two (it wasn't two before).

下雨了! — It's (started) raining!
Seen in 我的狗几点了
…什么？

什么 means 'what' and sits exactly where the answer would go — no word-order change is needed for a question.

你喝什么？ — What would you like to drink?
Seen in 在飞机上
…是谁？

To ask who someone is, put 谁 (who) right where the answer would be — the word order does not change.

这个人是谁？ — Who is this person?
Seen in 他是谁
…的时候 — when

Put 的时候 after a phrase to mean 'when / while …': 天气冷的时候 = when the weather is cold.

天气冷的时候，我爱喝热茶。 — When the weather is cold, I love drinking hot tea.
Seen in 我的杯子
…，好吗？ — making a suggestion

Add 好吗？ after a sentence to suggest something and ask if the other person agrees, like 'okay?' in English.

我们写汉字，好吗？ — Let's write Chinese characters, okay?
Seen in 写汉字
一点儿 — a little

一点儿 goes after the verb to soften the amount: 我会说一点儿汉语 = I can speak a little Chinese.

我喝一点儿水。— I drink a little water.
Seen in 我会说汉语
一起 + verb — doing something together

一起 goes before a verb to show two or more people do the action together.

我们一起回家。 — We go home together.
Seen in 谢谢你
一起 — doing something together

一起 means 'together' and goes right before the verb: 我们一起吃早饭 = We eat breakfast together.

我们一起吃早饭。 — We eat breakfast together.
Seen in 一起吃早饭
一起 — together

一起 is placed before the verb to show an action done jointly.

我们一起吃米饭。 — We eat rice together.
Seen in 回家我的电脑
不 + adjective

不 negates verbs and adjectives: 不舒服 means 'not well'.

今天我不舒服。
Seen in 我不舒服
不 A 也不 B — neither A nor B

A tidy pattern for two negatives: 不冷也不热 (neither cold nor hot), 不大也不小 (neither big nor small).

今天不冷也不热。 — Today is neither cold nor hot.
Seen in 今天的天气北京的四季
不 — negation

Put 不 before the verb to say 'not'. Before a fourth-tone syllable it is read bú, elsewhere bù.

中午他不回家。 — At noon he does not go home.
Seen in 爸爸工作
不在 + place — saying something is not somewhere

To say something isn't in a place, put 不 before 在: 钥匙不在桌子上 = The keys are not on the table.

钥匙不在桌子上。 — The keys are not on the table.
Seen in 找钥匙
不太 + adjective — not very

不太 softens a negative judgement. Because 太 is fourth tone, 不 is read bú here.

我写的字不太好。 — The characters I write are not very good.
Seen in 在学校学习写汉字
不客气 — replying to thanks

When someone thanks you, the standard reply is 不客气, meaning "you're welcome" or "no need to be polite."

不客气，我们是朋友。 — You're welcome, we're friends.
Seen in 谢谢你
不是 — to negate 是

不 goes right before 是 to say something is not the case.

米饭不是水果。 — Rice is not fruit.
Seen in 苹果和米饭
不能 + verb

能 means "can"; put 不 in front to say something is not possible.

我不能去商店了。 — I couldn't go to the shop anymore.
Seen in 下雨了
也 — 'also' goes before the verb

也 must sit right before the verb, never at the end of the sentence: 她说汉语,也说英语. Saying 她说英语也 is wrong.

我也喜欢看电影。 — I also like watching movies.
Seen in 我的老师
也 — also

也 sits right before the verb to add another true statement.

我喜欢苹果，也喜欢米饭。 — I like apples, and I also like rice.
Seen in 苹果和米饭大商店小商店小狗和小猫我的电脑 +1
也 — also, too

也 means 'also' and goes before the verb, right after the subject: 我们也吃鸡蛋 = We also eat eggs.

我们吃面包，也吃鸡蛋。 — We eat bread, and also eat eggs.
Seen in 一起吃早饭我爱你
了 at the end — a new situation

Sentence-final 了 says something has changed: 不多了 = there isn't much left now, though there was before.

现在我的钱不多了。 — Now I don't have much money left.
Seen in 我的钱
了 — completed action

Put 了 right after the verb to show the action is finished: 买了 = bought.

我买了一个大蛋糕。 — I bought a big cake.
Seen in 妈妈的生日请坐，请喝茶漂亮的衣服
什么 stays in place

In Chinese, the question word 什么 (what) sits exactly where the answer would go — no word-order change is needed.

这是什么？ — What is this?
Seen in 这是什么
今天是几月几号？ — asking the date

几 means "which/how many" in questions. 几月几号 literally asks "which month, which day" — the standard way to ask a date.

今天是几月几号？ — What's the date today?
Seen in 几月几号
今年多大 — how old are you this year

今年 (this year) plus 多大 (how big/old) is a common way to ask an adult's age.

你今年多大？ — How old are you this year?
Seen in 今年多大
会 + verb

会 means someone can do something because they learned it: 会说 = can say, 会写 = can write.

她会说很多数字。 — She can say lots of numbers.
Seen in 数字真有趣妈妈做的菜
会 + verb — learned ability

会 means 'can' for skills you have learned: 我会说汉语 = I can speak Chinese.

她会做菜。— She can cook.
Seen in 我会说汉语
会 / 不会, 喜欢 / 不喜欢 — more yes/no pairs

For most other verbs, just repeat the verb for yes, or put 不 before it for no: 会 becomes 不会 (can/cannot), 喜欢 becomes 不喜欢 (like/dislike).

你喜欢猫吗？不喜欢，我喜欢狗。 — Do you like cats? No, I like dogs.
Seen in 是不是
住在 + place

To say where someone lives, use 住在 followed directly by the place.

我住在北京。 — I live in Beijing.
Seen in 我住在哪儿
住在 + place — to live somewhere

住 (to live) takes 在 plus a place right after it to say where you live.

我住在北京。 — I live in Beijing.
Seen in 中国很大
你叫什么名字? — asking someone's name

To ask someone's name, say 你叫什么名字? The answer follows the pattern 我叫 + name.

你叫什么名字？我叫王小明。 — What's your name? My name is Wang Xiaoming.
Seen in 认识你很高兴
你呢?

呢 bounces the same question back to the other person: 'and you?'

我叫王小明,你呢?
Seen in 你叫什么名字
你好 — the everyday greeting

你好 (literally "you good") is the all-purpose way to say "hello" to anyone.

你好！ — Hello!
Seen in 你好
你好吗？ — asking how someone is

Adding 吗 after 你好 turns the greeting into a question asking how the other person is doing.

你好，你好吗？ — Hello, how are you?
Seen in 你好
你是哪国人? — asking someone's nationality

To ask where someone is from, say 你是哪国人? Answer with 我是 + country + 人.

你是哪国人？我是中国人。 — What's your nationality? I'm Chinese.
Seen in 认识你很高兴
几点 — asking what time

几点 means 'what time.' Put it after 是 to ask about a time: 火车是几点？= What time is the train?

我的火车是几点？ — What time is my train?
Seen in 坐火车
几点(了)？ — asking the time

几 asks 'how many'; 几点了？ = what time is it (now)? Answer with a number + 点: 八点了。

现在几点？— What time is it now?
Seen in 几点了
口 — the measure word for family members

When counting people in a family, Chinese uses 口 instead of 个: 四口人. Asking about it: 你家有几口人?

我家有四口人。 — There are four people in my family.
Seen in 我的家
叫 + name

Use 叫 to give your name: 我叫 + name.

我叫李明。
Seen in 你叫什么名字
叫 — to be called

叫 introduces a name: 我叫明月 = I'm called Mingyue. It also works as 'to call someone something'.

昨天她给我一个中国名字，叫“明月”。 — Yesterday she gave me a Chinese name: Mingyue.
Seen in 我的中国名字我的朋友
吗 — turning a sentence into a yes/no question

Add 吗 to the end of a statement to make a yes/no question: 你看见我的钥匙吗？= Have you seen my keys?

你看见我的钥匙吗？ — Have you seen my keys?
Seen in 找钥匙
听不懂 — can't understand (by listening)

听不懂 literally means "listen-not-understand": you hear the words but the meaning doesn't get through. 懂 means "to understand."

我听不懂。 — I don't understand (what I'm hearing).
Seen in 我听不懂
哪儿 (where)

哪儿 asks about a place and sits where the place word would go in the answer.

你住在哪儿？ — Where do you live?
Seen in 我住在哪儿
喂 — answering the phone

喂 (wèi) starts a phone call, like English "hello?". It is only used on the phone, not for greeting people in person.

喂，妈妈，你好吗？ — Hello, Mom, how are you?
Seen in 打电话
喜欢 vs 爱 — like vs love

喜欢 covers liking things, places and friends; 爱 is saved for the people closest to you. Saying 我爱苹果 for a fruit sounds odd — use 喜欢.

我喜欢朋友，我爱爸爸妈妈。 — I like my friends; I love my mom and dad.
Seen in 我爱你
回家 — to go home

回 means 'to return', and 家 means 'home'; together 回家 means to head home.

下午五点，爸爸回家了。 — At five in the afternoon, dad comes home.
Seen in 回家
在 + place (+ 上)

在 puts someone or something at a place, and 上 after the place means 'on/aboard': 在飞机上 = on the plane.

我现在在飞机上。 — I'm on the plane right now.
Seen in 在飞机上
在 + place + verb

To say where an action happens, put 在 + place before the verb, not after it.

我在家看电视。 — I watched TV at home.
Seen in 下雨了我的房间读书
在 + place + verb — where an action happens

In Chinese the place phrase comes before the verb: 在商店工作 (at-store work), the opposite order from English 'work at the store'.

我爸爸在商店工作。 — My dad works at a store.
Seen in 爸爸工作再见，老师
在 + place + verb — where you do something

Say where first, then what you do: 在学校学习 = study at school.

我在学校学习汉语。 — I study Chinese at school.
Seen in 在学校学习
在 + place + 上 — on top of something

在 marks the location, and 上 means 'on'; together 在电脑上 means 'on the computer'.

我在电脑上看电影。 — I watch movies on the computer.
Seen in 我的电脑
坐 + vehicle + 去

坐 means 'to take/ride' a form of transport. Pattern: 坐 + vehicle + 去 (+ place).

我坐出租车去。
Seen in 坐出租车
坐 + vehicle + 去 + place — how you travel

Chinese states the means of travel before the verb 去: 坐飞机去上海 = go to Shanghai by plane.

明天我坐飞机去上海。 — Tomorrow I am taking a plane to Shanghai.
Seen in 中国很大
坐 + vehicle — taking a form of transportation

坐 means 'to sit,' but with a vehicle it means 'to take/ride': 坐火车 = to take the train.

我要坐火车去北京。 — I want to take the train to Beijing.
Seen in 坐火车
多 + verb

多 before a verb means 'do more of it'.

请多喝水,多睡觉。
Seen in 我不舒服医院挂号
多大 — asking someone's age

多大 asks about age (literally 'how big'): 你今年多大？= How old are you this year?

你今年多大？ — How old are you this year?
Seen in 新同学
多少 — how many

Use 多少 to ask about a number, usually a big or unknown one. It goes right before the noun.

你有多少书？ — How many books do you have?
Seen in 读书
多少钱 — how much money

多少 asks about an amount; put it before 钱 to ask the price of something.

这个衣服多少钱？ — How much is this piece of clothing?
Seen in 漂亮的衣服
多少钱?

多少钱 is the standard way to ask the price: 'How much money?'

我问司机:“多少钱?”
Seen in 坐出租车
多少钱? alone — a quick, bare question

When it's already clear what you're paying for (like a taxi ride you just took), you can simply ask 多少钱? by itself, meaning "How much (is it)?"

我说：“多少钱？” — I say, "How much?"
Seen in 多少钱
多少钱? — asking the price

多少钱 = how much (money)? In spoken Chinese prices use 块 (kuài) instead of the formal 元: 三十八块.

这个多少钱?——十块。 — How much is this? — Ten kuai.
Seen in 买东西
多少钱一个？ — asking the price

To ask a price, say the thing + 多少钱 + measure: 苹果多少钱一个？ = How much is one apple?

苹果多少钱一个？ — How much is one apple?
Seen in 买水果
大 / 小 — older / younger

大 and 小 can describe age directly, without a comparison word, when comparing two people already mentioned.

她大，我小。 — She is older, I am younger.
Seen in 今年多大
太 + adjective + 了

太…了 wraps an adjective to say 'so…!' or 'too…', adding strong feeling: 太好吃了 = so delicious!

太好吃了！ — It's so delicious!
Seen in 妈妈做的菜太热了羊肉还是鱼
太…了 — so / too

Put an adjective between 太 and 了 to say something is 'so …' or 'too …'. It often shows strong feeling.

我的猫太好了！ — My cat is so great!
Seen in 我爱睡觉第一次吃火锅
家 + 有 + number + 口 + 人

口 is the measure word for counting family members.

我家有三口人。
Seen in 三口人吃饭
对不起 — apologizing

对不起 is the standard way to say "sorry" or "I apologize" in Chinese.

对不起，对不起！ — Sorry, sorry!
Seen in 对不起
开车 — to drive (a car)

开 (to operate) plus 车 (vehicle) together mean 'to drive'; it works like a normal verb before a destination.

我们开车去北京。 — We are driving to Beijing.
Seen in 开车去北京
很 + adjective

Chinese adjectives usually take 很 before them instead of using 是: 米米很小 = Mimi is small.

茶很热。— The tea is hot.
Seen in 我的猫星期天
很 + adjective — adjective predicate

很 plus an adjective forms the predicate; no separate word for "is" is needed.

那个商店很小。 — That store is small.
Seen in 大商店小商店小狗和小猫再见，老师
很高兴 + verb — happy to do something

很高兴 followed directly by a verb means 'happy to (do something)': 我很高兴认识她 = I am happy to meet her.

我很高兴认识她。 — I am very happy to meet her.
Seen in 新同学
想 + verb

想 before a verb means "want to / would like to"; 不想 means you don't feel like doing it.

我不想工作，我想喝水。 — I don't want to work; I want to drink water.
Seen in 太热了
想 + verb — to want to do something

想 sits right before another verb to say you want to do that action.

我们想去他家喝茶。 — We want to go to his home to drink tea.
Seen in 开车去北京
想 + verb — want to

想 before a verb means 'want to' or 'would like to' do something.

下午我想睡觉。 — In the afternoon I want to sleep.
Seen in 我爱睡觉
我不会说汉语 — saying what you can't do

不会 + verb means you don't know how to do something. 我不会说汉语 = I can't speak Chinese.

我不会说汉语。 — I can't speak Chinese.
Seen in 我听不懂
我爱你 — saying I love you

爱 is strong in Chinese. It is used for family and partners, not for things you merely enjoy: 我爱你 = I love you.

妈妈，我爱你。 — Mom, I love you.
Seen in 我爱你
打错 + 了 — doing something the wrong way

打错 means "dialed wrong"; adding 了 shows the mistake already happened.

你打错了。 — You dialed the wrong number.
Seen in 对不起
数字 + 岁 — number + years old

Put a number directly before 岁 to state an age; no verb is needed.

我今年二十岁。 — I am twenty years old this year.
Seen in 今年多大
是 / 不是 — yes/no with 是

Chinese has no single word for 'yes' or 'no.' To answer a question with 是, repeat 是 for yes, or say 不是 for no.

你是学生吗？是，我是学生。 — Are you a student? Yes, I am a student.
Seen in 是不是
是 — A is B

是 links two nouns: A 是 B means 'A is B'. Use it for identity, not for adjectives.

今天是妈妈的生日。 — Today is Mom's birthday.
Seen in 妈妈的生日
是…的 — pointing out who did it

Use 是…的 to stress who did something (or when, where, how it was done): 是妈妈买的 = it was Mom who bought it.

是我妈妈买的。 — It was my mom who bought it.
Seen in 我的杯子
有 / 没有 — yes/no with 有

To answer a question with 有 (to have), say 有 for yes or 没有 for no — 没有 is the special negative form of 有, not 不有.

你有猫吗？没有，我有狗。 — Do you have a cat? No, I have a dog.
Seen in 是不是
有 — there is / there are

有 introduces what exists in a place: place + 有 + thing.

中国有很多人。 — China has many people.
Seen in 中国很大开车去北京
有 — to have

有 means 'to have' or 'there is'. Its negative is 没有, never 不有.

我有一些钱。 — I have some money.
Seen in 我的钱
没关系 — replying to an apology

没关系 means "it doesn't matter" or "it's okay," the common reply when someone apologizes.

没关系，没关系。 — It's okay, it's okay.
Seen in 对不起
生病了 — expressing a change of state with 了

了 at the end of a sentence often shows a new situation has happened: 他生病了 = He has gotten sick (he wasn't sick before).

我的朋友生病了。 — My friend got sick.
Seen in 医院看朋友
的 for possession

Put 的 after a person to show that something belongs to them, like adding apostrophe-s in English.

那是我的书。 — That is my book.
Seen in 这是什么他是谁
的 — possession

的 goes between the owner and the thing owned: 妈妈的生日 = Mom's birthday.

今天是妈妈的生日。 — Today is Mom's birthday.
Seen in 妈妈的生日
的 — possessive

的 links the owner and the thing owned: 我的猫 = my cat, 我的椅子 = my chair.

我的朋友 — my friend
Seen in 我的猫我的中国名字
的 — possessive/attributive particle

的 links a noun to what comes after it, like 's in English.

大商店的东西很多。 — The big store's things are a lot (the big store has a lot of things).
Seen in 大商店小商店
给 + person + thing — giving something to someone

给 means 'to give.' The word order is subject + 给 + person + thing: 我给他水果 = I give him fruit.

我给他一些水果。 — I give him some fruit.
Seen in 医院看朋友
能 + verb — can, be able to

能 before a verb expresses ability: 我能听一点儿 (I can catch a little).

我能听一点儿汉语。 — I can understand a little spoken Chinese.
Seen in 看电影
认识 + person — to know / to have met someone

认识 describes knowing or having met a person, often followed by 很高兴 to express how glad you are.

认识老师，我很高兴。 — I'm very happy to have met the teacher.
Seen in 再见，老师
认识你很高兴! — nice to meet you

When you meet someone for the first time, say 认识你很高兴! (Nice to meet you!) 认识 means "to know" or "to meet" someone.

认识你很高兴！ — Nice to meet you!
Seen in 认识你很高兴
请 + verb — a polite request

请 before a verb softly asks someone to do something, like "please" in English.

我请朋友坐，请他喝茶。 — I ask my friend to sit, and offer him tea.
Seen in 请坐，请喝茶
请 + verb — polite requests

请 before a verb makes a request more polite, like "please" in English: 请说 = please speak/go ahead.

没关系，请说。 — It's okay, please go ahead and speak.
Seen in 我听不懂
谢谢 + 你 — thanking someone directly

谢谢 means "thank you"; adding 你 makes it more personal, thanking a specific person.

谢谢你！ — Thank you!
Seen in 谢谢你
谢谢 / 不客气 — thank you / you're welcome

This pair of set phrases is the standard exchange for thanks in Chinese.

朋友说谢谢，我说不客气。 — My friend says thank you, and I say you're welcome.
Seen in 请坐，请喝茶
这 / 那 + 是 + noun

Use 这 (this) and 那 (that) with 是 (to be) to point things out and say what they are.

这是我的杯子。 — This is my cup.
Seen in 这是什么
这个 / 那个 — this / that (+ measure word 个)

这 and 那 combine with the measure word 个 to point at a specific thing.

妈妈很喜欢那个漂亮的衣服。 — Mom really likes that pretty piece of clothing.
Seen in 漂亮的衣服
都

都 means 'all/both' and goes before the verb or adjective.

我们都很高兴。
Seen in 三口人吃饭
都 (all / both)

都 goes before the verb to say the whole group does or is something.

他们都是我的好朋友。 — They are both my good friends.
Seen in 他是谁
都 + verb

都 goes right before the verb and means 'all/both', covering everyone mentioned before it: 朋友都叫我 = my friends all call me.

朋友现在都叫我明月。 — Now my friends all call me Mingyue.
Seen in 我的中国名字
都 before the verb

都 (all) goes after the subject and before the verb or adjective: 同学们都很高兴。

同学们都很高兴。 — The classmates are all happy.
Seen in 我的学校
都 — all / both

都 goes right before the verb or adjective to say something is true of everyone mentioned.

我们都在家，我们很高兴。 — We are all at home, and we are very happy.
Seen in 回家
都 — both / all

都 comes after the things it covers and before the verb or adjective.

苹果和米饭都很好。 — Both apples and rice are good.
Seen in 苹果和米饭小狗和小猫
都 — both, all

都 goes after the subject and before the verb, not next to the noun the way English "both" does.

我们的生日都在九月。 — Both our birthdays are in September.
Seen in 几月几号
都 — both; all

都 comes after the subject and before the verb: 我们都很高兴 = we are both very happy.

他们都是学生。— They are all students.
Seen in 喝茶还是咖啡
