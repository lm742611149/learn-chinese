HSK 6 Reading Comprehension Questions 阅读理解

30 sample questions with answers, each taken from a free graded reading.
HSK 1HSK 2HSK 3HSK 4HSK 5HSK 6

Every HSK 6 reading on this site ends with three multiple-choice questions in English about what the text actually said. There are 153 of them across 51 readings; below is one from each of 30 readings, spread across topics. Try to answer before opening the answer, then read the text it came from.

These are comprehension checks written for learners, not official HSK exam items. The HSK 6 reading practice page has every reading.

  1. According to the text, what really happens after you buy a car of a certain brand?

    1. The brand actually sells more cars in your city
    2. Confirmation bias pulls your attention toward cars of the same model
    3. You start to regret your choice and notice better cars
    Answer

    B. Confirmation bias pulls your attention toward cars of the same model

    From 认知偏差 — Cognitive Bias

  2. Why is the word 缘分 hard to translate, according to the text?

    1. English has no ready-made equivalent, so a whole sentence is needed to explain it
    2. It is an archaic word that even native speakers no longer use
    3. Its pronunciation cannot be written in the Latin alphabet
    Answer

    A. English has no ready-made equivalent, so a whole sentence is needed to explain it

    From 翻译的艺术 — The Art of Translation

  3. How does the text describe the science fiction of the 1950s and 60s?

    1. It was full of optimism and saw technology as a stairway to the future
    2. It was preoccupied with climate collapse and surveillance
    3. It refused to write about outer space at all
    Answer

    A. It was full of optimism and saw technology as a stairway to the future

    From 科幻的黄金时代 — A Golden Age of Sci-Fi

  4. What does the ecologist in the text compare species to?

    1. Bricks in a wall
    2. Rivets on an aircraft wing
    3. Books in a library
    Answer

    B. Rivets on an aircraft wing

    From 生物多样性 — Biodiversity

  5. According to the text, what does the idea of 和为贵 lead many employees to do in meetings?

    1. Argue with the boss until the problem is solved
    2. Stay silent rather than contradict the boss in public
    3. Refuse to attend meetings altogether
    Answer

    B. Stay silent rather than contradict the boss in public

    From 儒家与现代职场 — Confucianism and the Modern Workplace

  6. What does the text observe about the longest queues in the galleries?

    1. They form at the oldest object in the room
    2. They form at the most photogenic piece rather than the most important one
    3. They form at the ticket office rather than inside
    Answer

    B. They form at the most photogenic piece rather than the most important one

    From 博物馆热 — The Museum Boom

  7. Why did the Dunhuang Academy decide to build digital archives?

    1. Because visitors complained that the caves were too dark
    2. Because the caves were about to be closed forever
    3. Because the fading and flaking of the murals cannot be reversed
    Answer

    C. Because the fading and flaking of the murals cannot be reversed

    From 敦煌的守护者 — The Guardians of Dunhuang

  8. According to the text, what makes habits frightening?

    1. Their effects are nearly invisible in the short term and only show years later
    2. They are expensive to maintain over a lifetime
    3. They can never be changed once formed
    Answer

    A. Their effects are nearly invisible in the short term and only show years later

    From 习惯的复利 — The Compound Interest of Habits

  9. When does the text say Chinese speakers stop being vague?

    1. When signing contracts and negotiating prices
    2. When talking with close friends
    3. When speaking with foreigners
    Answer

    A. When signing contracts and negotiating prices

    From 汉语的模糊之美 — The Beauty of Vagueness in Chinese

  10. What does the record-store example in the passage illustrate?

    1. How algorithms improve music recommendations
    2. How a chance encounter in the past could spark a new interest or even a career
    3. How record stores used to organize their inventory
    Answer

    B. How a chance encounter in the past could spark a new interest or even a career

    From 算法与偶然性 — Algorithms and Serendipity

  11. According to the author, why do individual choices still matter?

    1. Because they shape what comes to be seen as normal
    2. Because they directly account for most emissions
    3. Because they save households a great deal of money
    Answer

    A. Because they shape what comes to be seen as normal

    From 气候变化与责任 — Climate Change and Responsibility

  12. What does the writer friend do every morning?

    1. He locks his phone in a drawer and writes for three hours straight
    2. He answers all his emails before starting to write
    3. He goes for a long run to clear his mind
    Answer

    A. He locks his phone in a drawer and writes for three hours straight

    From 深度工作 — Deep Work

  13. What does the text suggest we should treat history as, rather than merely dates to memorize?

    1. A set of moral lessons to be debated
    2. A subject best left to professional historians
    3. Evidence that countless ordinary people truly lived
    Answer

    C. Evidence that countless ordinary people truly lived

    From 历史的温度 — The Warmth of History

  14. According to the text, what is contentment?

    1. Giving up all ambition and effort
    2. Pretending to be happy with very little
    3. Knowing what is worth chasing and what is merely vanity's burden
    Answer

    C. Knowing what is worth chasing and what is merely vanity's burden

    From 知足常乐 — Contentment Brings Joy

  15. Instead of saying 不行, what does the text say Chinese speakers often reply?

    1. 绝对没问题
    2. 我尽量 or 到时候再说
    3. 请再问一次
    Answer

    B. 我尽量 or 到时候再说

    From 委婉语 — Euphemism

  16. Who first carried Chinese web novels into the English-speaking world, according to the text?

    1. Translation communities formed spontaneously by amateur translators
    2. Major international publishing houses
    3. Government-funded cultural exchange programs
    Answer

    A. Translation communities formed spontaneously by amateur translators

    From 网络文学出海 — Web Literature Goes Global

  17. After more than seventy years of making sushi, what is Jiro Ono still pondering?

    1. How to get the temperature of the rice exactly right
    2. How to open more restaurants overseas
    3. How to train a successor to take over his shop
    Answer

    A. How to get the temperature of the rice exactly right

    From 匠人精神 — The Craftsman Spirit

  18. How does the text say Chinese talks about months?

    1. As the month above and the month below, like a river flowing downward
    2. As locations in front of and behind the body
    3. As a circle that repeats every year
    Answer

    A. As the month above and the month below, like a river flowing downward

    From 语言与思维 — Language and Thought

  19. In the compressed-day metaphor, how much of the day does human civilization occupy?

    1. About half the day
    2. Less than the final second
    3. Roughly the last hour
    Answer

    B. Less than the final second

    From 人类的未来 — The Future of Humanity

  20. According to the text, where does the heart of the gene-editing dispute lie?

    1. In whether humans have the standing to rewrite life's instruction manual
    2. In whether the technology actually works
    3. In which country announced the experiment first
    Answer

    A. In whether humans have the standing to rewrite life's instruction manual

    From 基因编辑之争 — The Gene-Editing Debate

  21. Why does the text mention rivers sealed in concrete?

    1. To show that forcing things against their nature can backfire, as such rivers flood more easily
    2. To argue that concrete is too expensive for city budgets
    3. To praise modern engineering for eliminating floods
    Answer

    A. To show that forcing things against their nature can backfire, as such rivers flood more easily

    From 道法自然 — The Way Follows Nature

  22. Why was flaunting one's brilliance frowned upon in the traditional mindset?

    1. It looked uncultivated and attracted jealousy, like the bird that sticks its head out
    2. The law punished officials who boasted
    3. It was believed to bring bad luck to one's family
    Answer

    A. It looked uncultivated and attracted jealousy, like the bird that sticks its head out

    From 谦虚的文化根源 — The Cultural Roots of Modesty

  23. According to the text, what is delayed gratification really about?

    1. Repressing every desire you feel
    2. Avoiding pleasure entirely
    3. Knowing that some rewards are worth trading patience for
    Answer

    C. Knowing that some rewards are worth trading patience for

    From 延迟满足 — Delayed Gratification

  24. What does the text say true, healthy nostalgia should look like?

    1. Refusing to accept that the past is over
    2. Carrying the warmth of the past while calmly heading toward an unknown future
    3. Rejecting all forms of remembering the past
    Answer

    B. Carrying the warmth of the past while calmly heading toward an unknown future

    From 怀旧为何流行 — Why Nostalgia Is Popular

  25. What does the writer suggest is the more useful question to debate?

    1. Which country should reach Mars first
    2. Whether astronauts should be paid more
    3. Not whether to go, but how to go and who decides
    Answer

    C. Not whether to go, but how to go and who decides

    From 太空探索值得吗 — Is Space Exploration Worth It?

  26. What do many people mistakenly associate the Mean with?

    1. Courage and extremes
    2. Mediocrity and fence-sitting
    3. Careful heat control in cooking
    Answer

    B. Mediocrity and fence-sitting

    From 中庸之道 — The Doctrine of the Mean

  27. According to the text, what was the real point of Italy's slow food movement?

    1. To ban fast-food restaurants from city centers
    2. To remind people that the quality of life cannot be measured by speed alone
    3. To prove that traditional cooking is healthier than modern food
    Answer

    B. To remind people that the quality of life cannot be measured by speed alone

    From 快与慢的辩证 — The Dialectic of Fast and Slow

  28. What does neuroscience research say happens to a brain long accustomed to skimming?

    1. It remembers more small details
    2. It becomes better at multitasking
    3. It gradually loses the patience for sustained concentration
    Answer

    C. It gradually loses the patience for sustained concentration

    From 碎片化阅读之弊 — The Cost of Fragmented Reading

  29. In the drawing experiment, what happened to the rewarded children weeks later?

    1. They drew better pictures than the unrewarded group
    2. They no longer wanted to draw once the prizes stopped
    3. They started demanding bigger and bigger prizes
    Answer

    B. They no longer wanted to draw once the prizes stopped

    From 内驱力 — Intrinsic Motivation

  30. According to the closing line, why is a great vessel finished late?

    1. Because its maker keeps getting distracted
    2. Because it is so great
    3. Because society's clock runs slow
    Answer

    B. Because it is so great

    From 大器晚成 — Late Bloomers
